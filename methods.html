How this was verified

Provenance, audit protocol, and what the verdicts do and do not mean

Where this material came from

This catalog is derived from a deep-research document produced by a large language model (ChatGPT deep research) in July 2026, covering open methodological, theoretical, practical, data-related and interpretive problems in target trial emulation.

That origin is the reason this page exists. A machine-generated research agenda is a plausible-sounding artifact whether or not it is correct, and publishing one as settled fact would be a disservice. Nothing from the source document appears on this site without a verification verdict attached, and the evidence behind each verdict is shown on the entry itself.

The source document is kept as working material and is not published here.

One thing worth recording about the source

The source was supplied in three formats. The markdown export had lost every citation. ChatGPT’s markdown export replaces each link with private-use-area characters and an opaque handle, so the exported file contained 125 citation tokens, zero DOIs, zero URLs, and zero PubMed identifiers. The .docx of the same document retained the real hyperlinks.

Extraction therefore runs from the .docx, not the export. That distinction is not cosmetic: had the markdown been used, every entry in this catalog would have been unsourced, and the audit would have had nothing to check a citation against.

What was checked mechanically

Before any of the substantive audit, every resolvable identifier in the source was checked against its registry.

The source carries 48 numbered references pointing at 35 distinct works.

Outcome Count What it means
Resolved to a real work 21 Identifier and title confirmed against CrossRef, NCBI or arXiv
Guidance or software 7 Regulatory guidance, reporting standards and package documentation, correctly cited by URL
Unresolved 7 The publisher page blocks automated access, and no search result matched the prose context closely enough to record

The seven unresolved references are listed with "confidence": "none" in the working files and are not cited anywhere in this catalog. A reference that cannot be confirmed is left out rather than guessed at; an invented citation would discredit every other entry here.

The audit

Every problem carries a set of atomic, falsifiable claims extracted from the source. Those claims, not the prose around them, are what was tested.

Literature verification

For each problem, an agent with web and PubMed access checked that each cited work actually supports the claim attributed to it, and searched for later work that would resolve or narrow the problem.

Adversarial refutation

A second agent was instructed to refute the proposition that the problem is open, and to default to “not open” when uncertain. Asking an agent to confirm a claim and asking it to break the claim produce different results; only the second is informative.

Independent external auditors

Frontier models outside this system were given the same claims, blind to each other and to the literature pass, each with a different lens:

  • a technical and source-code lens, which checks identification arguments, weight algebra, cloning and censoring bookkeeping, and immortal-time accounting, and which verifies any claim about a software package against that package’s pinned source rather than against the report;
  • a recency and prior-art lens, which adopts an inverted prior. It assumes each problem has already been solved and goes looking for who solved it, because an agenda that lists a solved problem as open is the most damaging error this site can make: the people most likely to read it are the people who solved it;
  • a third independent lens on the same claims, so that a majority is a majority of more than two.

Each entry’s page lists the auditors that voted on it. Coverage is not uniform, because external models run out of quota mid-run; where an entry was reached by fewer auditors, the page says so rather than presenting a thinner audit as an equal one.

Adjudication

Opinions become a published verdict by a fixed rule, not by judgment. The rule records its own decision path on every entry, so the route from votes to verdict is visible.

Two principles govern it:

A “solved” vote without a locator is not evidence. It is downgraded rather than counted. An auditor asserting that something has been solved must name the work that solved it.

Evidence beats votes. A lone dissenter holding an unrebutted verbatim quote is not silently outvoted by auditors holding opinions.

Majority rules carry a denominator. A verdict such as Overstated requires at least two independent auditors and at least half of the auditors that actually returned an opinion on that entry. Using the roster size as the denominator would let an entry reached by two auditors be decided on the same evidence as one reached by four.

Which fields are audited, and which are editorial

This distinction matters and is easy to lose.

Audited. The statement, why it is open, the prior work, and the extracted claims. These went through literature verification, adversarial refutation and the independent auditors, and they carry the verdict.

Editorial. Maturity, tractability, severity, the proposed research direction, and the protocol-component and data-setting tags. These are one reader’s judgment applied to make the catalog browsable. They were not audited and should not be cited as findings. The proposed direction in particular reads with the same visual authority as the audited statement above it, and it is a research suggestion rather than a verified claim.

Deliberately rebuilt. Priority is not carried over from the source. It reflects three observable things: whether the problem blocks a decision-grade estimand, whether any method addresses it at all, and whether any software implements it.

What the verdicts do not mean

A verdict is a judgment about a claim, not about a problem’s importance.

Overstated almost never means the problem is fictional. It usually means the underlying difficulty is real and the source pitched its severity, or the claim that no method exists, more strongly than the evidence supports. The entry records the strongest version of the claim that survived.

Confirmed open means the checks agreed and no counterevidence surfaced. It does not mean nobody is working on it, and it is a statement about the literature that was reachable, not about the literature that exists.

Unverifiable means the auditors disagreed with evidence on both sides, or that no accessible source could settle it. The disagreement is shown on the entry rather than resolved silently.

Limits of this exercise

This is not a systematic review. There was no protocol registration, no duplicate independent screening with a reported agreement statistic, and no formal risk-of-bias assessment.

The literature harvest covers PubMed and PubMed Central only. Conference abstracts and journals indexed in Embase but not PubMed are missing. Embase has no API available without a subscription.

The auditors are language models. They are better at finding a paper that closes a gap than at proving no such paper exists, so Confirmed open carries less weight than Resolved since report. A confirmed absence of evidence is the weakest thing on this site.

Reproducing this

Everything is in the repository. build/render_site.mjs turns the registry into these pages, build/make_audit_batches.mjs produces the auditor prompts, and build/adjudicate.mjs applies the rule. documentation/audit/calibration.json holds known-answer cases verified by hand; any audit configuration that fails to reproduce them is miscalibrated, and they are asserted before the site is built.
