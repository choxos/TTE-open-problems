Eligibility criteria built from healthcare-use variables can induce selection through colliders

Bites on: Eligibility
Seen in: EHR Claims Linked records

Statement

An eligibility criterion is a conditioning statement, and conditioning on a variable that is a common effect of treatment and outcome opens a path rather than closing one. Emulation protocols routinely build eligibility from healthcare-use variables such as a required prior visit, a recorded baseline laboratory value or a minimum enrollment period, all of which can be descendants of the treatment decision or of early disease. Nothing in the current workflow checks an eligibility definition for this property, and the reporting checklist asks what the criteria were rather than what conditioning on them does.

Why it is open

Whether a criterion is a collider depends on the causal structure, which is what the study is trying to learn, so the check cannot be purely empirical. Graph-learning algorithms recover only a Markov equivalence class and are further limited by latent confounding, so they can flag candidates and cannot certify. Removing a criterion also changes the target population, which means the correction has a cost that must be argued clinically rather than statistically.

What has been tried

Work What it contributes
Hernán MA, Robins JM. Using big data to emulate a target trial when a randomized trial is not available. Am J Epidemiol 2016;183(8):758-764 Requires eligibility to be specified as a trial would specify it, which is the discipline that makes the collider question askable at all.
Hansford HJ, Cashin AG, Jones MD, et al. Transparent reporting of observational studies emulating a target trial: the TARGET statement. JAMA 2025 Requires the eligibility criteria and their emulation to be reported, without requiring an assessment of the selection they induce.

Probable solution or research direction

Build a causal-discovery eligibility auditor that uses graph learning and temporal rules to flag proposed criteria that are post-treatment, that are colliders, or that encode healthcare use, and treat its output as advisory input to a domain review rather than as an automatic exclusion. Evaluate it on generated graphs with known structure, scoring collider-warning sensitivity, false-positive rate, expert agreement and, most importantly, whether acting on the warnings reduces bias. Never allow an algorithm to define eligibility by maximizing the apparent treatment effect, since that is a search over estimands dressed as a design step.

Our study

NoteA study is designed

Question. Across 18 unique generated distributions and two analyst-information views, how large are bias and coverage distortions from conditioning on a prior healthcare-use variable under prespecified noncollider and collider DAGs; how much of the restricted-versus-broad contrast is target-population change; and does a complete, prespecified screen-and-response policy improve estimation of the restricted-population 60-month marginal risk difference relative to screen-free alternatives? This study answers only the conditional mechanism and policy-performance part of ELG-02. It does not determine whether an unknown real-world eligibility criterion is a collider.

Verification

Verdict. Confirmed open. Auditors found the problem open. At least one qualified how it is stated; the qualification and who made it are in the trail below.

Literature and prior-art check
open / supported-with-caveat. The claim that nothing checks an eligibility definition for collider structure holds, and adjacent work is closer than the entry suggests. A 2026 npj Health Systems paper addresses eligibility-criteria-induced temporal selection directly, and machine-learning trial-eligibility emulation has been used to evaluate the generalizability of oncology trial results. A 2026 Frontiers in Medicine study uses emulation to evaluate eligibility criteria themselves in asthma and COPD. These evaluate the consequences of criteria rather than auditing them for collider structure before the analysis, which is what the entry proposes, so the specific artifact is still absent. Cites 10.1038/s44401-026-00082-3: Treats selection induced by eligibility criteria as the object of study, which is the mechanism the entry names.. Cites 10.3389/fmed.2026.1863026: Uses emulation to evaluate eligibility criteria, showing the criteria are becoming an object of analysis rather than a fixed input..
GPT-5.6 Sol, technical and source-code lens
open / overstated. Conditioning on an eligibility variable opens a noncausal path only when that variable is a collider, or a relevant descendant of one, in the assumed causal structure. Being a healthcare-use variable is not sufficient. The stated treatment-to-eligibility-to-outcome timing is also imprecise: eligibility fixed before time-zero treatment cannot be caused by that subsequent treatment or its subsequent outcome. The relevant graph may instead involve prebaseline treatment decisions, incipient disease, or common causes of treatment and outcome. Causal discovery does not universally return only a Markov equivalence class; what can be oriented depends on temporal, interventional, functional-form, and latent-confounding assumptions. It nevertheless cannot certify collider status from unrestricted observational data. An advisory tool for identifying possible temporal or collider problems remains an open development problem, but the asserted medium-high severity is context dependent rather than general.
Grok 4.5, recency lens
open / supported. Inverted prior: treat collider-inducing eligibility as already audited in routine TTE workflows, then search for a method or tool that does so. Found conceptual and design literature, not a solution. Hernán and Robins (2016) already require that eligibility not use postbaseline events; that is design discipline, not an automated check of whether a healthcare-use criterion is a collider. Weiskopf et al. (2023, JAMIA) explicitly frame healthcare utilization as a common collider in EHR reuse and warn that conditioning on utilization (by selection or adjustment) opens non-causal paths; Digitale et al. (2023, J Clin Epidemiol) likewise describe collider-conditioning bias when restricting to people in care or with a disease history. Wang et al. (2026) restate that conditioning eligibility on utilization induces collider bias at cohort entry. These works strengthen that the mechanism is real; they do not supply a certified check. TARGET (Hansford et al., 2025) requires reporting of eligibility criteria and their emulation, not assessment of the selection graph those criteria induce; the entry’s reading of TARGET is accurate. Practical reviews (e.g., Tran et al., 2026) continue to document post-treatment eligibility as a frequent high-risk design error, which is evidence the gap is still live in applications. No retrieved paper or package implements a causal-discovery or temporal-rule eligibility auditor for proposed TTE criteria with evaluation on known graphs (sensitivity, false-positive rate, expert agreement, bias reduction when acting on warnings). Federated causal discovery and general DAG tools exist in adjacent literature but are not integrated into TTE protocol design or reporting checklists. Graph learning recovering only a Markov equivalence class under latent confounding remains a structural limit, as the entry states; formalizations of that limit do not close the problem. Queries run: collider + eligibility + target trial (2024–2026); healthcare utilization collider + eligibility; TARGET checklist collider/selection; causal discovery eligibility auditor TTE; CRAN/GitHub TrialEmulation, WeightIt, MatchIt, cobalt for eligibility-collider features (none found). Open as a workflow and tooling problem; prior art names the bias and forbids postbaseline criteria but does not solve systematic detection of healthcare-use colliders in eligibility definitions.
GLM-5.2, third independent lens
open / supported. The claim is coherent and proportionate. It accurately describes how conditioning on healthcare-use variables for eligibility can induce collider bias. The reasons given for the problem being open are structurally sound: the causal structure is the target of inference, graph-learning algorithms can only flag candidates and cannot certify due to latent confounding, and removing a criterion changes the estimand. This is a claim about a real structural difficulty and current practice limitations, not an overstatement of impossibility.

How the evidence falls in time

54 papers in the reviewed corpus bear on this problem, published 2011 to 2026. Every paper found here asserts the problem is open; none reports progress.

This problem is recurrent: three or more papers spanning at least eight years assert it independently. Sustained restatement by authors who mostly do not cite each other is the strongest evidence this reading can offer that a gap is real rather than one group’s framing.
