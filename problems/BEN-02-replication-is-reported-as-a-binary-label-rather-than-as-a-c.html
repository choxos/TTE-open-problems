Statement

Agreement between an emulation and a trial is informative only when the estimands, populations and measurements are close enough to be comparable, and better design alignment is empirically associated with stronger concordance. Benchmarking nonetheless reports success or failure as a binary label, usually derived from whether the confidence intervals overlap or whether the direction agrees. That label mixes estimand mismatch with random error: two studies estimating the same quantity with wide intervals will agree by that criterion, and two estimating different quantities precisely will disagree. The reported statistic therefore does not measure what its users take it to measure.

Why it is open

Constructing a graded measure requires deciding how much estimand difference is tolerable, which is a judgment the binary label exists to avoid. Prediction-interval coverage would be the natural criterion and requires a distributional claim about the trial’s own error that the trial does not publish. The label is also convenient for the audiences who cite benchmarking results, so there is little pressure to replace it.

Probable solution or research direction

Report the ratio of effects, the absolute effect difference, standardized agreement and prediction-interval coverage together, so that estimand mismatch and random error are separately visible. Use positive and negative controls alongside the trial comparison, and prefer prospective forecasts of a trial’s result to retrospective comparisons, since only the former is uncontaminated by knowing the answer. Replicate across databases as well as across questions, because a data source’s calibration is what a decision-maker actually needs to know.

Our study

A binary replication label changes with precision alone, and one operator carries almost all of it

The question put to it. BEN-02 says replication is reported as a binary label that mixes estimand mismatch with random error. Holding the true trial-emulation discrepancy fixed, how much of the label is decided by precision rather than by the discrepancy, and which of the operators in use carries that sensitivity?

What it found. For the operators tested, yes, and it is concentrated in one of them. The sensitivity S is the larger of the two operator-specific values at 0.4915, against a threshold of 0.10 declared before the run, with a 95 percent Monte Carlo interval of 0.4903 to 0.4931; the lower endpoint clears the threshold by roughly 538 Monte Carlo standard errors. Confidence-interval overlap supplies that value; direction agreement moves by 0.1244 over the same range. Two benchmark pairs carrying the same true discrepancy can therefore be labelled agreeing and disagreeing on the strength of how precisely each was estimated, and the operator most used to produce the label is the most exposed.

Results.

  • The label moves with precision at fixed truth. Holding the true risk-difference discrepancy constant and varying only trial and emulation precision moves the probability of an agreement verdict by 0.49 on the interval-overlap operator, against a threshold of 0.10 set in the protocol before any result existed.
  • The exposure is not shared evenly. Direction agreement moves by 0.1244 over the same range, a quarter of the interval-overlap figure. An operator choice that is usually made for convenience decides most of the conflation.
  • It is not an estimation artifact. Coverage runs 0.944 to 0.956 against a nominal 0.95, maximum absolute bias is 0.00086, convergence is complete in every scenario, and the exact finite-cell summation used for truth agrees with itself to 2.2e-16. Nothing here is an estimator failing.
  • The bootstrap interval is narrow enough to matter. The Monte Carlo interval on S spans 0.0028, so the distance from the 0.10 threshold is not a Monte Carlo question.

Design.

Estimand
The probability that a pair-level agreement operator returns agreement, as a function of precision at a fixed true discrepancy, and the sensitivity S of that probability to precision alone
Methods compared
direction agreement, confidence-interval overlap, trial-standardized agreement, joint-null agreement
Data-generating mechanism
16 structural scenarios crossing the true risk-difference discrepancy with trial and emulation precision, 10,000 replicates each, 480,000 benchmark pairs. Each replicate is one trial cohort and one emulation cohort with a binary outcome, scored by four agreement operators in current use. Truth is enumerated by exact summation over the covariate strata rather than by simulation, so the discrepancy each scenario carries is known to machine precision.
Replicates
10,000 per scenario, 480,000 pairs, 0 replicate errors

What this does not answer. S is the maximum over the operators tested, so the result is an at-least-one-operator claim: it establishes that interval overlap is materially precision-sensitive and that direction agreement is less so, not that every binary replication label is. It holds for these two illustrative operators, these 16 fixed scenarios and these three nested precision levels. This is the pair-level component of BEN-02 and not the entry as a whole: it says nothing about between-pair heterogeneity across a benchmarking program, about calibration of a graded metric against a decision, or about the graded alternatives the entry’s own audit established already exist, all three of which the design review named as out of scope and which are declared limitations rather than results. Outcomes are binary at a single horizon, so the behavior of these operators on a time-to-event scale is untouched.

Verification

Verdict. Overstated. 2 independent auditors judged the claim as stated too strong. Binary replication labels based on interval overlap or effect direction discard magnitude and uncertainty and cannot diagnose why estimates differ. Decision-grade benchmarking should first align estimands, then report paired contrasts with joint uncertainty, heterogeneity, and prespecified tolerances whenever a categorical judgment is required.

Literature and prior-art check
open / supported-with-caveat. The entry criticizes binary replication labels. The concordance meta-analysis it draws on does not use one: it reports correlation and standardized-difference agreement and relates them to design features, which is the graded reporting the entry asks for. RCT-DUPLICATE similarly reports several agreement metrics. So the criticism lands on the citing practice rather than on the benchmarking literature, and the entry should say that. The part that survives is that a graded criterion still does not separate estimand mismatch from random error, since prediction-interval coverage requires a distributional claim about the trial’s own error that trials do not publish. Cites 10.1001/jama.2023.4221: Reports multiple agreement metrics rather than a single binary label.. Cites 10.1136/bmj-2025-086810: Reports correlation and standardized agreement and relates both to design features, which is graded rather than binary..
GPT-5.6 Sol, technical and source-code lens
partially-solved / overstated. The criticism of confidence-interval overlap and sign agreement is technically sound, but the problem is framed too broadly. The agenda’s own descriptions say that the cited benchmarks already report several graded metrics, including effect correlation and standardized differences, so benchmarking is not uniformly binary. Continuous effect differences, ratios, and standardized contrasts still cannot separate estimand mismatch from random error. That separation first requires commensurate estimands and then a joint sampling model incorporating uncertainty from both estimates, their covariance when applicable, and between-pair heterogeneity. Prediction-interval coverage does not require a trial to publish a full error distribution when an estimate and standard error or confidence interval are available, and coverage is itself calibrated across a suite rather than diagnosed from one pair. Ratios are additionally unstable when the reference effect is near zero. This is therefore partly an implementation and reporting problem with existing tools, not wholly an unresolved statistical-method problem; high severity is disproportionate unless the binary label is the sole basis for a decision. Cites 10.1001/jama.2023.4221: As characterized in the batch, this program reports several agreement metrics rather than only a binary replication label, partially resolving the reporting problem while not separating all sources of discrepancy.. Cites 10.1136/bmj-2025-086810: As characterized in the batch, this analysis reports effect correlation and standardized-difference agreement across pairs and evaluates design-related variation, partially addressing graded comparison..
Grok 4.5, recency lens
partially-solved / overstated. The claim that benchmarking “nonetheless reports success or failure as a binary label” is not faithful to the field’s primary programs or to the report’s own cited works. RCT-DUPLICATE and Franklin-line methods report regulatory/significance agreement, estimate agreement, and standardized-difference agreement together; the BMJ 2026 meta-analysis leads with Pearson correlation, ICC, ratio of ratios, standardized-difference agreement, and Bland–Altman, and only secondarily with binary metrics. Köppe, Micheloud, Erdmann, Heyard, and Held (2025) go further: they replace the two-trials (regulatory agreement) rule with the graded sceptical p-value, which uses both p-values, effect sizes, and relative precision, and invert it to combined confidence intervals; they apply it to 29 RCT-DUPLICATE pairs. ISPOR Europe 2024 also floated enhanced multi-metric RCT–RWE agreement assessments for HTA. What remains open is universal adoption, explicit estimand alignment (how much estimand difference is tolerable), and routine prediction-interval coverage that treats trial sampling error as a distribution. Binary labels are still convenient for secondary citation and still appear beside continuous metrics, but they are not the sole or best existing practice. The report’s proposed direction (ratio of effects, absolute difference, standardized agreement, prediction-interval coverage, prospective forecasts) is largely what the better literature already does or is building. Cites 10.1001/jama.2023.4221: Already reports multiple agreement metrics (not a single pass/fail label) across 32 prespecified emulations; this is the report’s own cited “most careful existing treatment of the labeling question.”. Cites 10.1136/bmj-2025-086810: Primary reporting is continuous and multi-metric (correlation 0.59/0.83, ratio of ratios, standardized-difference rates, design-feature regressions), not a binary success label. Directly undercuts the claim that benchmarking only reports binary agreement.. Cites arxiv:2412.09334: Supplies a graded, calibrated alternative to binary two-trials/regulatory agreement (the sceptical p-value), depending on both p-values, effect sizes, and relative precision; extends to non-inferiority and inverted CIs; applied to RCT-DUPLICATE. Partially delivers the report’s call for calibrated comparison rather than a binary label.. Cites 10.1186/s12874-025-02589-z: Peer-reviewed publication of the sceptical p-value application to RWE emulations (same work as arXiv 2412.09334), confirming the graded metric is in the methods literature, not only a preprint..
GLM-5.2, third independent lens
open / supported. The claim is internally consistent and proportionate. It accurately characterizes a limitation in current practice: benchmarking often relies on binary labels (e.g., CI overlap) that conflate estimand mismatch with random error. The stated reasons for it being open (the difficulty of defining tolerable estimand differences and the convenience of binary labels) logically support the persistence of this practice. The claim is appropriately scoped as a critique of current reporting standards rather than an impossibility claim.

How the evidence falls in time

75 papers in the reviewed corpus bear on this problem, published 2010 to 2026. Partial progress is on the record, and later work still calls this open. Both belong in view.

This problem is recurrent: three or more papers spanning at least eight years assert it independently. Sustained restatement by authors who mostly do not cite each other is the strongest evidence this reading can offer that a gap is real rather than one group’s framing.

It is also being closed in pieces: partial results come from more than one paper in more than one year, each covering a different part.
