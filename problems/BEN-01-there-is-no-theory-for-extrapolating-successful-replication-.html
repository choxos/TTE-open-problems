Statement

Agreement between an emulation and a randomized trial raises confidence, and disagreement can arise from emulation failure, random error, different adherence, different measurement, different populations, or a limitation of the trial itself. RCT-DUPLICATE and the 2026 concordance meta-analysis both show better agreement under closer emulation and neither shows equivalence: across 107 paired emulations and trials the effect correlation was 0.59 overall and 0.83 among the 63 pairs judged to emulate more closely. What no one has is a theory that licenses the inference from those results to a new question: how much confidence a successful replication of trial A in database D transfers to question B in database D, or to question A in database E. Publishing after the trial result is known also creates design-calibration and selective-reporting risks that the concordance statistic cannot see.

Why it is open

The benchmarking set is not a random sample of questions: trials get emulated when they are recent, large, and have an accessible active comparator, which are the conditions under which emulation works best. A concordance statistic is therefore an estimate of performance on a favorable subpopulation, with no design that would identify performance elsewhere. Attributing a discrepancy requires knowing which of six candidate causes produced it, and the data that would separate them, particularly the trial’s own adherence and measurement, are typically not available to the emulator.

What has been tried

Work What it contributes
Wang SV, Schneeweiss S, RCT-DUPLICATE Initiative. Emulation of randomized clinical trials with nonrandomized database analyses: results of 32 clinical trials. JAMA 2023;329(16):1376-1385 The largest prespecified benchmarking program: 32 trials emulated in claims data with agreement reported against the trial results.
Concordance between target trial emulation and randomised controlled trials: systematic review and meta-analysis. BMJ 2025 Pools 107 emulation-trial pairs, reporting correlation 0.59 overall and 0.83 among closer emulations, and links poorer concordance to baseline imbalance, hospital-initiated treatment and inferior outcome emulation.

Probable solution or research direction

Run prospective, blinded benchmarking suites in which the emulation protocol and code are frozen and registered before the trial result is revealed, which removes design calibration by construction rather than by assurance. Model concordance hierarchically by data source, clinical setting, outcome type and degree of protocol match, so the quantity estimated is a conditional expectation of agreement rather than a global rate. Treat benchmarking as calibration evidence about a data source and a design class, never as certification of a method.

Verification

Verdict. Overstated. 2 independent auditors judged the claim as stated too strong. A successful replication identifies calibration only for the evaluated trial, protocol, and database. Quantitative transfer to another question or database requires a prespecified target performance estimand, an explicit selection or transport model, conditional exchangeability, and overlap in relevant modifiers; outside that support, transfer is model-dependent rather than identified.

Literature and prior-art check
partially-solved / supported-with-caveat. The entry proposes prospective, blinded benchmarking in which the protocol is frozen before the trial reports. That design now has at least one instance: a 2026 Circulation paper emulates a reference trial to predict the results of COBRRA before the trial’s result was available. So the proposal is not untried and the entry should say so. The theoretical claim survives intact: nothing licenses the inference from a successful replication in one database to a new question or a new database, and the concordance meta-analysis reports determinants of emulation success without supplying that extrapolation. One correction the entry must carry: the meta-analysis it cites has a published erratum, so any figure quoted from it needs checking against the corrected version. GPT-5.6 Sol, technical and source-code lens
open / overstated. The narrow identification concern is correct: performance on selected trial-database pairs does not nonparametrically identify performance for a new question or database without a defined target distribution, a sampling or selection model, conditional exchangeability of benchmark performance, and positivity over the relevant design and setting modifiers. The assertion that there is “no theory” or “no design” is too absolute. Transport and hierarchical prediction are possible under explicit assumptions, although a hierarchical model cannot identify extrapolation outside observed support merely by conditioning on study features. Correlation is also not an agreement measure: it can be high despite systematic bias and low because of restricted effect variation or sampling error. Freezing the protocol prospectively blocks outcome-informed design adaptation only if all analytic choices are locked; it does not by itself prevent selective reporting after unblinding. The problem remains open in its narrower form because the supplied agenda does not provide an identified and validated transfer procedure. Its very-high severity is warranted only when benchmarking is used to certify decisions beyond the benchmarked design class.
Grok 4.5, recency lens
partially-solved / overstated. Inverted prior favors solutions that license transfer of successful A-in-D replications to B-in-D or A-in-E. BenchExCal (Wang et al. 2025) is exactly that transfer procedure for indication expansion: benchmark an existing-indication trial in the database, then calibrate a subsequent database study for the expanded question using the observed RCT–database differences. Kirchgesner et al. (2024) state a related trial-calibration approach for questions RCTs never answered. Heyard et al. (2024) and the BMJ 2026 concordance meta-analysis already move from a global agreement rate toward design-conditional agreement (protocol match, baseline balance, outcome quality, claims vs other sources). Prospective prediction projects (GRADE, semaglutide phase IV, FDA prediction workstream) partially remove design-calibration by freezing protocols before trial readout. None of this is a completed, general statistical theory that quantifies how much confidence transfers to arbitrary new questions or databases, and BenchExCal’s empirical evaluation is still planned (simulation plus five examples), not finished. The six-cause attribution problem for single discrepancies remains open. Saying there is “no theory” is therefore too strong; the gap is the lack of a validated general theory and completed prospective hierarchical calibration, not a blank slate. Cites 10.1002/cpt.3621: Proposes an operational theory for transferring a successful existing-indication emulation (question A in database D) into a calibrated second-stage emulation for an expanded indication (question B in D), with simulation and five planned empirical evaluations. Directly addresses the report’s missing transfer step, though as proposal plus planned evaluation rather than finished validation.. Cites 10.1002/pds.70008: Articulates trial calibration as the route from benchmarked emulation of an answered question to RWE on a related unanswered question, the same transfer structure the problem says is missing.. Cites 10.1136/bmjmed-2023-000709: Models concordance variation as a function of design and population emulation differences rather than a single global rate, which is the hierarchical/conditional direction the report proposes.. Cites 10.1136/bmj-2025-086810: Estimates design-conditional concordance (closer emulation; baseline imbalance; hospital-initiated treatment; outcome emulation quality; claims data) across 107 pairs, so agreement is already treated as a conditional quantity, not only a global rate. Does not solve transfer to unselected questions..
GLM-5.2, third independent lens
open / supported. The claim is internally consistent and proportionate. It correctly identifies a structural gap: existing benchmarking provides empirical concordance on a favorable subpopulation but lacks a formal theory for extrapolating this to new questions or databases. The reasons given (non-random sampling of trials, multiple confounding causes for discrepancies) directly support why the problem remains open. The claim is appropriately scoped as a limitation of current methodological frameworks rather than an impossibility.

How the evidence falls in time

55 papers in the reviewed corpus bear on this problem, published 2014 to 2026. Partial progress is on the record, and later work still calls this open. Both belong in view.

This problem is recurrent: three or more papers spanning at least eight years assert it independently. Sustained restatement by authors who mostly do not cite each other is the strongest evidence this reading can offer that a gap is real rather than one group’s framing.

Related problems

  • BEN-02: Replication is reported as a binary label rather than as a calibrated comparison of estimands
  • DTA-01: Data-fitness assessment is narrative and has no thresholds that would stop a study
  • EST-05: An emulation enters a synthesis with randomized trials through a discrepancy parameter that nothing identifies
  • REG-01: Regulators have no thresholds for adequate emulation, acceptable residual confounding or sufficient sensitivity analysis
