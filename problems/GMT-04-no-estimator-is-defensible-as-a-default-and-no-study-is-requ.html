No estimator is defensible as a default, and no study is required to show that its choice mattered

G-methods: specification, estimation & inference
High priority
Confirmed open
The available estimators differ in what they assume rather than only in how they perform. Active-comparator new-user designs with regression or propensity adjustment handle baseline initiation and not treatment-confounder feedback. Matching gives a transparent population, discards information, and often leaves its estimand unstated. Marginal structural models represent regime effects directly and are unstable under extreme weights. The parametric g-formula supports flexible estimands and is sensitive to sequential outcome-model misspecification. G-estimation avoids modeling the treatment-free outcome distribution and is technically demanding. GMT-04 Confirmed open High priority Established Tractability 5/5

Statement

The available estimators differ in what they assume rather than only in how they perform. Active-comparator new-user designs with regression or propensity adjustment handle baseline initiation and not treatment-confounder feedback. Matching gives a transparent population, discards information, and often leaves its estimand unstated. Marginal structural models represent regime effects directly and are unstable under extreme weights. The parametric g-formula supports flexible estimands and is sensitive to sequential outcome-model misspecification. G-estimation avoids modeling the treatment-free outcome distribution and is technically demanding. Longitudinal targeted learning is doubly robust, computationally heavy and sensitive to practical positivity. No estimator is defensible as a universal default, and a study reporting one reports a number whose dependence on that choice is unknown: nothing requires the choice to be justified against the estimand and the data structure, and nothing requires a prespecified alternative targeting the same contrast.

Why it is open

Comparing estimators multiplies the analysis cost and can produce disagreement the paper then has to explain, which is a strong disincentive. There is no accepted way to report disagreement between estimators targeting one estimand under different nuisance assumptions: it is neither a sensitivity analysis in the usual sense nor a robustness check. Simulation evidence comparing the families is fragmented across papers with different generating mechanisms, so no synthesis exists that would justify a default even if one were wanted.

What has been tried

Work What it contributes
Robins JM, Hernán MA, Brumback B. Marginal structural models and causal inference in epidemiology. Epidemiology 2000;11(5):550-560 The reference for weighted estimation of regime effects, one of the three families a comparison would have to include.
ltmle: Longitudinal Targeted Maximum Likelihood Estimation. CRAN Implements longitudinal TMLE, IPTW and g-computation in one place, which makes the three-family comparison feasible in a single analysis.
lmtp: Non-Parametric Causal Effects of Feasible Interventions Based on Modified Treatment Policies. CRAN Implements targeted and sequentially doubly robust estimators for point and longitudinal modified treatment policies.
Su L, et al. TrialEmulation: an R package to emulate target trials for causal analysis of observational time-to-event data. arXiv 2024 Provides the sequence-of-trials implementation with intention-to-treat and per-protocol analyses.

Probable solution or research direction

Require that the prespecified estimator be justified against the exact estimand, data structure, overlap and nuisance assumptions, and that at least one prespecified alternative targeting the same contrast be reported where feasible, so estimation sensitivity is visible without mandating three families in every study. Build a public benchmark with realistic observation processes and missingness on which the families are compared under a common generating mechanism, turning the fragmented simulation evidence into a synthesis. Score methods on failure awareness as well as bias and coverage: an estimator returning a narrow interval in a deliberately non-identifiable scenario has failed the test that matters most.

Verification

Verdict. Confirmed open. Auditors agreed the problem is open and no counterevidence surfaced.

The source said. “The available estimators differ in what they assume rather than only in how they perform. Active-comparator new-user designs with regression or propensity adjustment handle baseline initiation and not treatment-confounder feedback. Matching gives a transparent population and discards information, with an estimand that is often left unstated. Marginal structural models represent regime effects directly and are unstable under extreme weights. The parametric g-formula supports flexible estimands and is sensitive to sequential outcome-model misspecification. G-estimation avoids modeling the treatment-free outcome distribution and is technically demanding. Longitudinal targeted learning is doubly robust and computationally heavy and sensitive to practical positivity. A study reporting one of these reports a number whose dependence on that choice is unknown, and nothing requires that at least one weighting, one outcome-modeling and one doubly robust estimator be compared on the same data.”

What was wrong with it. The proposed requirement was too strong. Every emulation need not compare all three estimator families; what a study owes is a justification of its prespecified estimator against the exact estimand, data structure, overlap and nuisance assumptions, with prespecified alternatives targeting the same contrast where that is feasible.

Literature and prior-art check
open / supported-with-caveat. The claim that no estimator is defensible as a default is well supported by the fragmentation the corpus shows, and the proposed remedy is partly in progress. Benchmarking of data-adaptive against investigator-specified confounding adjustment exists, and a 2026 evaluation compares statistical and deep learning methods for bias in a heart failure emulation. Neither supplies the public benchmark under a common generating mechanism that the entry asks for, and neither establishes a requirement that each study compare a weighting, an outcome-modelling and a doubly robust estimator. The overstatement is small: the entry’s implication that no comparative evidence exists is wrong, while its claim that no synthesis or requirement exists holds. GPT-5.6 Sol, technical and source-code lens
not-a-problem / overstated. The absence of a universally preferred estimator is expected because estimator choice must follow the estimand, intervention, longitudinal structure, overlap and plausible nuisance assumptions. It is not itself a methodological defect, and a mandatory three-family comparison is neither necessary nor sufficient for validity. Alternative estimators are informative only when they genuinely target the same intervention, population, censoring handling and effect measure. Their disagreement is reasonably described as sensitivity to estimation assumptions, although it does not identify which estimate is correct; agreement can reflect shared identification failure. Several method summaries are too broad. Baseline active-comparator adjustment is inadequate for sustained per-protocol strategies with treatment-confounder feedback, but can be appropriate for a treatment-policy effect of initiation. Matching need not always discard observations, and its estimand depends on the matching design. The parametric g-formula models the longitudinal covariate and outcome law, not merely an outcome regression. Double robustness belongs to specified targeted estimators under stated nuisance conditions, not longitudinal targeted learning without qualification. TrialEmulation 0.0.4.11 is source-supported: its NAMESPACE exports trial_sequence and expand_trials, and its DESCRIPTION states that it estimates intention-to-treat and per-protocol effects. I abstain on the cited ltmle and lmtp capabilities because neither package is vendored under documentation/refs/packages/.
Grok 4.5, recency lens
open / supported. No estimator family is established as a default for longitudinal target-trial emulation of sustained strategies, and no current reporting or protocol standard requires a three-family comparison (weighting, outcome modeling, doubly robust) on the same data. Prior art that does not close the gap: (i) simulation comparisons of g-computation, IPTW/OW, and TMLE exist but are fragmented across data-generating mechanisms and often point-treatment or externally controlled trial settings (for example Schuler and Rose; Ren et al. 2023); (ii) software already makes multi-family analysis feasible (ltmle implements LTMLE, IPTW, and g-computation; lmtp implements sequential DR and related estimators; TrialEmulation covers sequence-of-trials ITT/PP), which lowers cost but does not create a requirement or a shared benchmark; (iii) TARGET 2025 (JAMA/BMJ) is a reporting guideline for target-trial emulations and does not mandate multi-estimator families; HARPER and STaRT-RWE structure protocols and sensitivity analyses without requiring weighting-plus-outcome-plus-DR comparison under a common estimand. No public continuous-time or informative-visit benchmark synthesizing the fragmented simulations was found. The problem’s claim about incentives against multi-estimator reporting remains accurate. Searches: comparing IPTW g-formula TMLE longitudinal target trial multi-method 2024 2025; TARGET checklist multiple estimators; HARPER STaRT-RWE sensitivity g-methods; Schuler Rose TMLE IPW g-computation.
glm
open / supported-with-caveat. The claim is largely coherent and the reason given for openness (cost of comparison, no accepted reporting framework for disagreement, fragmented simulation evidence) genuinely supports it. However, the headline assertion ‘no estimator is defensible as a default’ is stronger than the body of the statement warrants. The body argues that estimators differ in assumptions and that no synthesis exists to justify a universal default, which is defensible. But ‘no estimator is defensible as a default’ could be read as claiming that in no specific setting is any estimator a reasonable default, which would be overstated—there are settings where, given known structure, one family is clearly preferred. The intended reading is that no single estimator is defensible as a universal default across the heterogeneous landscape of longitudinal causal problems, and on that reading the claim holds. The severity is proportionate: the cost of a wrong default estimator choice is a biased or unstable causal estimate presented without context.
