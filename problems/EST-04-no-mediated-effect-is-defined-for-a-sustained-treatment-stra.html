None of them holds an intermediate variable, so a mediator enters an emulation only as an analysis appended after the total effect is estimated. Statement

The target trial protocol has seven components: eligibility, treatment strategies, assignment, follow-up, outcome, causal contrast and analysis plan. None of them holds an intermediate variable, so a mediator enters an emulation only as an analysis appended after the total effect is estimated. The methods borrowed for that appendix are stated for a randomly assigned point or fixed longitudinal exposure with the mediator assessed at planned visits: the mediational g-formula for time-varying exposures and mediators, interventional effects for multiple mediators, path-specific effects for repeatedly measured mediators under time-varying confounding, and a 2026 extension of the last to competing risks. None is stated for a sustained strategy realized by cloning, artificial censoring and inverse-probability-of-censoring weights, nor for a sequential emulation in which one person contributes to many trials, and in both of those designs the population followed under each strategy is constructed by the analysis rather than given. Applied emulations report mechanism regardless, as a proportion mediated on a hazard or subdistribution-hazard scale: 2.4 percent and 6.5 percent of the semaglutide effect on adult-onset seizure through hemoglobin A1c, 8.7 percent of the cardiovascular benefit of SGLT2 inhibitors through regression of steatotic liver disease. A proportion mediated on a hazard scale is not a ratio of counterfactual risk contrasts and matches none of the estimands the identification results cover. Three objects are missing and each is nameable: a mediated estimand defined for a sustained strategy, an identification result for it when censoring is artificial, and a protocol component that requires the mediator, its measurement times and its intercurrent-event strategy to be fixed before the analysis.

Why it is open

Two requirements conflict and no estimand satisfies both. Natural direct and indirect effects carry the mechanistic reading and need a cross-world independence that no experiment can check, and that fails outright once a confounder of the mediator and the outcome is affected by treatment, which is the ordinary situation across follow-up. Interventional effects drop the cross-world condition and are identified from a single world, and they fail the sharp mediational null: an interventional indirect effect can be nonzero when no individual has any mediated effect at all, so a nonzero estimate does not license the sentence about mechanism it is written to support. Emulation adds an obstacle the trial setting does not have, because under cloning and artificial censoring the variable that mediates is frequently the same variable that triggers deviation from the strategy, so it enters the censoring weight and the mediation formula at once and the weighting already conditions on part of the mediator path. The mediator is also recorded when a patient attends care rather than on a schedule, so the repeated mediator history that every longitudinal identification result assumes is itself selectively observed. Nobody has worked the join out because the applied demand is met far more cheaply: point-treatment mediation software runs on the weighted cohort and returns a percentage.

Stat Med 2019;38(24):4828-4840 Identifies a path-specific effect through a repeatedly measured mediator under time-varying confounding, for a randomly assigned treatment with the mediator measured at planned visits, and states that the effect along all pathways intercepting one or more mediators is not identified without overly stringent assumptions.

Vansteelandt S, Daniel RM. Interventional effects for mediation analysis with multiple mediators. Epidemiology 2017;28(2):258-265 Defines interventional direct and indirect effects for multiple mediators, identified without a cross-world condition, which are the estimands an emulation would have to adopt if it wanted identification from a single world.
Miles CH. On the causal interpretation of randomised interventional indirect effects. J R Stat Soc Series B Stat Methodol 2023;85(4):1154-1172 Shows that randomized interventional indirect effects do not satisfy the sharp null criterion without stronger assumptions, so a nonzero estimate is compatible with no individual-level mediation; this is the half of the conflict that the applied switch to interventional effects does not resolve.
Moreno-Betancur M, Moran P, Becker D, Patton GC, Carlin JB. Mediation effects that emulate a target randomised trial: Simulation-based evaluation of ill-defined interventions on multiple mediators. Stat Methods Med Res 2021;30(6):1395-1412 Defines interventional effects explicitly as contrasts in a target trial of hypothetical interventions on several mediators, estimated by g-computation; the closest existing template, and the trial it specifies is a trial of mediator interventions rather than an emulation of a sustained treatment strategy.
Domingo-Relloso A, Zhang Y, Wang Z, et al. A path-specific effect approach to mediation analysis with time-varying mediators and time-to-event outcomes accounting for competing risks. Stat Med 2026;45:e70425 Adds competing events to path-specific mediation with repeatedly measured mediators by treating the competing event as a nested mediator, with a mediational g-formula algorithm and simulation evidence.
Leigh L, Hinwood M, Oldmeadow C, et al. Which factors mediate the effect of childhood socioeconomic disadvantage on mental health in young adulthood? A protocol for a target trial emulation using linked administrative data from New South Wales, Australia. BMJ Open 2026;16(3):e109541 An emulation protocol that prespecifies an interventional mediation analysis across several mediators, which is what practice looks like when the mediator is fixed in the protocol rather than added afterward.
Eun Y, Bong S, Koh HY, et al. Semaglutide and risk of adult-onset seizure: a target trial emulation. Neurology 2026;107(1):e218174 Applies a longitudinal path-specific framework derived for a randomized trial to a weighted emulation in electronic health records and reports mediation through hemoglobin A1c as 2.4 percent and 6.5 percent.

Probable solution or research direction

Make the mediator a protocol component: name the intermediate variable, the times at which it is to be assessed, and the strategy for every intercurrent event that can precede it, so the mechanism question is fixed alongside eligibility and treatment strategies instead of being chosen once the total effect is known. Derive interventional direct and indirect effects for a sustained strategy under cloning and artificial censoring, and state what the estimand becomes when the mediator history enters the censoring weight; the tractable first step is a worked identification result for one time-varying mediator under a static strategy with a grace period, which is the design most emulations already run. Report the two mediator regimes as counterfactual risks at a stated horizon, on the same scale as the total effect the emulation already reports, rather than as a proportion mediated on a hazard scale. State for whichever effect is used whether it satisfies the sharp mediational null, so a reader can tell whether a nonzero estimate is evidence about mechanism or evidence about a distributional shift.

Literature and prior-art check
open / supported-with-caveat. Searched the 1,376-record screened corpus rather than answering from memory. The emulation-specific half of the entry survives. Mediation terms ANDed with cloning, artificial censoring or censoring weights return four records, and none of them is a method: two are applied emulations that use clone-censor-weight and mention mediation in passing, one is a drug-wide screen, one is a 2013 collider-stratification example. Mediation ANDed with sustained or dynamic strategies or grace periods returns two records, both applied. The phrase proportion mediated returns zero records in the whole screened corpus, so the applied instances the entry cites sit outside the methodological literature entirely, which is consistent with the entry’s point that the reporting practice has outrun the estimand. Nothing in the corpus proposes an intermediate variable as a protocol component. The caveat is that the entry frames the estimand problem as a conflict between two families, natural effects needing a cross-world condition and interventional effects failing the sharp mediational null, and a third family exists in the corpus and was constructed for exactly that dilemma. Separable effects decompose the treatment rather than intervene on the mediator, and were defined for a longitudinal mediator with a survival outcome in 2018, generalized to time-varying structures with weaker identification conditions in 2021, and given g-formula identification conditions for longitudinal mediation in 2024. A reader given the entry as written would not learn that the escape route exists. The general-methods half of the entry is also weaker than the corpus supports: the mediational g-formula for time-varying exposures, mediators and confounders sits in the corpus from 2016 and 2017 with a parametric algorithm and released software, which the entry acknowledges in its rationale but its statement does not.
GPT-5.6 Sol, technical and source-code lens
partially-solved / overstated. The valid core is narrower than the statement. Reporting a proportion mediated from hazard or subdistribution-hazard ratios does not generally yield a fraction of a counterfactual risk effect, and applying point-treatment mediation software after clone-censor weighting does not generally identify a longitudinal mediated effect. The sharp-null criticism of randomized interventional indirect effects is also valid. However, fixed longitudinal exposure histories are static sustained strategies, and existing mediational g-formula work already defines direct and indirect effects and gives longitudinal identification results under sequential exchangeability and positivity. Cloning and artificial censoring are estimation devices, not new interventions or populations. Conditioning an artificial-censoring model on mediator history measured before censoring can correct selection; it does not itself condition the causal contrast on that mediator. When the mediator also determines a dynamic treatment rule, the intervention ordering and path-specific estimand require explicit definition, but this is not proof that no mediated estimand exists. Repeated participation in sequential trials primarily changes the target distribution, dependence structure, and variance calculation, not whether mediation counterfactuals can be defined. Likewise, an eighth universal protocol component is unnecessary because mediator interventions, measurement times, causal contrasts, intercurrent-event handling, and analysis can be prespecified within the existing strategy and analysis-plan components. The assertion that treatment-induced mediator-outcome confounding makes cross-world independence fail outright is too strong: it generally defeats standard nonparametric identification rather than logically forcing every possible cross-world restriction to be false. A genuine gap remains for defensible implementation when grace-period or dynamic strategies interact with the mediator, mediator visits are informative, and clone-censor and mediator-process weights must be combined. That is a specialized extension and reporting problem, not a missing foundation for all sustained strategies. It is critical only when mechanism is a decision-grade objective; it does not invalidate an otherwise valid total-effect emulation. No named package capability is asserted here, so no package claim was adjudicated. Cites doi:10.1097/EDE.0000000000001555: Defines direct and indirect effects for longitudinal exposure histories and supplies nonparametric identification results. Static sustained regimes are included in that class, although clone-specific estimation and informative observation remain unresolved.. Cites doi:10.1177/0962280221998409: Shows that mediator interventions and mediation contrasts can be specified explicitly as a target trial and estimated by g-computation, contradicting the claim that the standard seven-component template forces mediation to be appended post hoc..
Grok 4.5, recency lens
open / supported. Inverted-prior search for a mediated estimand, identification result, or software path under sustained strategies realized by cloning, artificial censoring and IPCW, or under sequential trial emulation, did not turn up a resolving paper or package. Highest-yield check was the report’s own prior_work: Vansteelandt et al. 2019 (path-specific, randomly assigned treatment, planned mediator visits), Tai et al. 2023 (mediational g-formula with multiple time-varying mediators and R code), Vansteelandt and Daniel 2017 (interventional effects), Miles 2023 (interventional effects fail the sharp mediational null), Moreno-Betancur et al. 2021 (target trial of mediator interventions, not a treatment-strategy emulation under cloning), Domingo-Relloso et al. 2026 (path-specific plus competing risks for repeatedly measured mediators), Leigh et al. 2026 (protocol that prespecifies interventional mediation), and the applied TTE reports Eun et al. 2026 and Jang et al. 2026 (proportion mediated on hazard or subdistribution-hazard scales). Those works formalize or apply mediation for point, fixed longitudinal, or naturally time-varying exposure processes with planned or modeled mediator visits; none states an estimand or identification formula for a strategy population constructed by cloning and artificial censoring, nor for one person contributing to many sequential emulations. Broader 2024–2026 searches across AJE/Epidemiology/IJE/Stat Med/Biometrics-type venues, arXiv stat.ME, and medRxiv for combinations of mediation or path-specific or interventional indirect effects with cloning, clone-censor-weight, artificial censoring, sustained strategy, sequential trial emulation, or grace period returned applied TTEs that append mediation after a total effect, methods primers that list mediation and CCW as separate topics, and classic time-varying mediation (Lin et al. 2017 mediational g-formula for time-varying exposures; Zheng and van der Laan-type longitudinal mediation) that still assume an observed exposure process rather than an analysis-constructed strategy cohort. Package surface checks for TrialEmulation, gfoRmula, gfoRmulaICE, lmtp, ltmle, WeightIt, MatchIt, and related tools did not surface a merged feature for mediated contrasts under CCW or sequential emulation. TARGET 2025 (Cashin et al., JAMA) still organizes reporting around the standard target-trial protocol components and does not add a required mediator, measurement-time, and intercurrent-event block. The closest adjacent proposal is Gonzalez-Perez, Wanis, Sarvet, and Stensrud (arXiv:2508.10702, 2025), which encodes separable effects for sustained treatments with identifying functionals and doubly robust estimators; that is a path-type decomposition of treatment components under sustained use, not classical mediation through a measured intermediate (for example HbA1c) when that intermediate also drives artificial censoring weights. Separable-effects theory therefore does not close the three nameable gaps in the statement: a mediated estimand for a sustained strategy under cloning or sequential emulation, identification when the mediator history enters the censoring weight, and a protocol component that fixes the mediator before analysis. Miles 2023 further keeps the natural-versus-interventional interpretability conflict open rather than solved. GLM-5.2, third independent lens
open / supported. The claim is internally consistent and well-scoped. It identifies a genuine structural gap: existing mediation estimands (natural effects, interventional effects, path-specific effects) are developed for randomized exposures with scheduled mediator visits, not for sustained strategies implemented via cloning, artificial censoring, and IPCW. The reason given for openness is structural, not merely about current practice: (1) natural effects require cross-world independence that fails when treatment affects mediator-outcome confounders; (2) interventional effects sacrifice the sharp mediational null, so nonzero estimates do not license mechanistic claims; (3) emulation introduces a join problem where the mediator may simultaneously trigger censoring and enter the mediation formula, and the mediator history is selectively observed at care visits rather than on schedule. These are real difficulties, not inconveniences. The claim correctly avoids impossibility language—it says ‘nobody has worked the join out,’ framing this as an absence of work rather than a structural barrier. The critique that proportion-mediated on a hazard scale is not a ratio of counterfactual risk contrasts is mathematically correct and proportionate. The cited works (Vansteelandt 2019, Tai 2023, Vansteelandt & Daniel 2017, Miles 2023, Moreno-Betancur 2021, Domingo-Relloso 2026) genuinely do not address sustained strategies under cloning/censoring; Moreno-Betancur 2021 is the closest but specifies a trial of mediator interventions, not a sustained treatment strategy. The Leigh 2026 protocol prespecifies mediation but does not derive the identification result. The applied papers (Eun 2026, Jang 2026) report percentages without naming estimands, which is consistent with the claim. The severity (High) is proportionate: these percentages appear in clinical journals and may be read as mechanistic evidence. One limitation: the absence claim (‘nobody has worked the join out’) is inherently difficult to verify without search, but the structural arguments for why it remains open are sound and the cited literature does not fill the gap.
