Statement

The observational analogue of an intention-to-treat effect contrasts assigning initiation now against not initiating now. Two things then happen that do not happen in a randomized trial. Assignment is confounded, so the contrast requires baseline adjustment to mean anything. And the comparator group does not stay untreated: patients assigned to no initiation now frequently initiate later, so with longer follow-up the two strategies converge and the estimated effect attenuates toward the null for reasons that have nothing to do with the treatment’s efficacy. The attenuation is a function of the follow-up horizon and of local prescribing behavior, and it is rarely reported as such, so an emulation with long follow-up and a null result cannot be distinguished from one where the treatment does not work.

Why it is open

The convergence is a real feature of the strategy contrast rather than a bias to be removed, so it cannot be adjusted away without changing to a per-protocol estimand that carries its own assumptions. Reporting it requires publishing the treatment trajectories in both arms over follow-up, which most papers do not, and the rate of convergence depends on the health system and calendar period rather than on anything intrinsic. There is also no convention for choosing the horizon at which the contrast is still meaningful.

Probable solution or research direction

Report the cumulative incidence of initiation in both arms over follow-up alongside the effect estimate, so a reader can see how far the strategies remained distinct. Prefer strategy contrasts that name the later initiation explicitly, such as initiate now versus initiate on a stated clinical trigger, over an open-ended no-initiation arm that the data will not sustain. Quantify by simulation how much attenuation a given convergence rate produces at each horizon, so that a null result can be read against the convergence that was observed rather than against an assumption of none.

Our study

A single cumulative-initiation number at the horizon does not determine the attenuation it is asked to explain

The question put to it. CNF-01 asks authors to publish the cumulative incidence of initiation in both arms so a reader can see how far the contrasted strategies stayed distinct, and to quantify by simulation how much attenuation a given convergence rate produces. Does that diagnostic actually recover the attenuation?

What it found. No. Within the simulated grid, comparator-arm cumulative initiation at 120 months does not uniquely determine attenuation: matched early and late initiation scenarios, whose reported diagnostic agrees to within 0.0005, differ in attenuation by 0.0528 to 0.2027. Nine of the twelve matched pairs exceed the 0.05 threshold declared before the run by at least three Monte Carlo standard errors, at 5 to 46 of them; the other three sit within 3 and are individually inconclusive, and all three are at the lowest convergence rate, where there is least attenuation to misattribute. A single cumulative-incidence value at the horizon is therefore insufficient for recovering attenuation in these scenarios. What the reported number cannot see is when the comparator arm initiated and who initiated.

Results.

  • The insufficiency scales with convergence, which is the opposite of reassuring. At 20 percent cumulative initiation the matched difference is 0.053 to 0.070 and only one of four pairs is established; at 45 percent it is 0.116 to 0.145 at 18 to 27 Monte Carlo standard errors; at 70 percent it is 0.181 to 0.203 at 39 to 46. The diagnostic is least informative exactly where attenuation is largest.
  • Attenuation is large and grows with the horizon. Pooled across scenarios its median is 0.024 at one year and 0.245 at ten, with a maximum of 0.567. A treatment that genuinely works can lose more than half its sustained-strategy effect to convergence alone.
  • Who initiates matters as much as when. Concentrating initiation in high-risk patients raises attenuation from 0.476 to 0.567 at the highest convergence rate while also inflating the reported diagnostic from 0.700 to 0.742, so the diagnostic moves in the right direction by the wrong amount.
  • Effect persistence is the one factor that barely matters. Transient and legacy regimes differ by less than 0.01 in attenuation throughout.
  • The estimators show small finite-sample bias rather than none. Coverage runs 0.934 to 0.970 against a nominal 0.95 and convergence is complete, but 14 of 288 cells carry bias beyond three Monte Carlo standard errors, to a maximum of 4.2. That does not touch the decisive comparison, which uses attenuation enumerated from the mechanism at two million individuals per scenario rather than estimated attenuation.

Design.

Estimand
Risk difference at horizons of 1 to 10 years for assignment to initiate at baseline versus not, with later initiation left to run, against the always-treated versus never-treated contrast the comparator arm would have supported had it stayed untreated
Methods compared
g-formula standardization, stabilized IPTW
Data-generating mechanism
Discrete monthly time to 10 years, 4000 individuals per replicate, confounded baseline initiation, later initiation in the comparator arm, and a treatment that works. 24 scenarios crossing three convergence rates, two timing shapes tuned to the same cumulative initiation at the horizon, initiation that does or does not concentrate in high-risk patients, and two effect-persistence regimes. 1000 replicates each, 24,000 replicates in all. Truth enumerated from the mechanism at two million individuals per scenario rather than estimated from the replicates.
Replicates
1000 per scenario, 24,000 replicates, 288,000 estimate rows

What this does not answer. The comparison holds the diagnostic fixed only at 120 months. At earlier horizons the early and late scenarios differ in observed cumulative initiation by as much as 0.269, so those contrasts are not comparisons at equal C(h) and cannot independently establish insufficiency there. The study establishes that C(120) alone is insufficient within this grid. It does not establish that reporting C is necessary, that the full initiation curve would be sufficient, or that a universal conversion from convergence to attenuation exists. The comparator arm converges upward and the treated arm never discontinues, so this is one direction of convergence only. There is no loss to follow-up and no competing event, both excluded so the attenuation is not confounded with a second mechanism. The scale is the risk difference throughout; a hazard ratio would mix this mechanism with non-collapsibility.

Verification

Verdict. Overstated. 2 independent auditors judged the claim as stated too strong. Methods for handling treatment switching exist and have been reviewed; emulations do not conventionally report initiation trajectories in both arms, so a reader cannot judge how far the contrasted strategies remained distinct over follow-up.

4 of the 4 independent auditors returned an opinion on this entry.

Literature and prior-art check
partially-solved / overstated. The entry treats comparator convergence as an unhandled feature of the observational intention-to-treat analogue. Treatment switching is a well-developed area with its own methods and its own scoping review. A 2025 Clinical Pharmacology and Therapeutics scoping review catalogs statistical methods to adjust for treatment switching in real-world studies and offers a decision framework for choosing among them. A 2026 American Journal of Epidemiology paper warns specifically about how switchers are handled in pharmacoepidemiologic effect estimation, and a 2024 BMC Medical Research Methodology paper applies g-methods to switching in the ALTA-1L trial. The entry’s implicit claim that the analyst has no options is wrong. What survives is narrower and still real: reporting the cumulative incidence of initiation in both arms is not conventional, so a reader cannot see how far the strategies stayed distinct, and no convention exists for choosing the horizon at which the contrast remains meaningful. Cites 10.1093/aje/kwaf044: Addresses the specific analytic hazard of switchers in effect estimation.. Cites 10.1186/s12874-024-02437-6: Applies g-methods to switching and examines the sensitivity of the result to specification..
GPT-5.6 Sol, technical and source-code lens
partially-solved / overstated. The valid core is that a baseline-assignment, treatment-policy analogue permits subsequent initiation, so increasing crossover can reduce exposure separation and may dilute the assignment effect. This is part of that estimand, not confounding bias to be adjusted away. However, crossover also occurs in randomized trials, so it is not uniquely observational. Convergence of current treatment status does not imply convergence of cumulative exposure histories, and attenuation toward the null with longer follow-up is not guaranteed: its direction and magnitude depend on treatment timing, effect dynamics, switching mechanisms, competing events, and the effect scale. A convergence rate alone therefore cannot determine an attenuation factor. Nor does a null estimate become categorically indistinguishable from biological inefficacy; treatment trajectories, alternative sustained or dynamic-strategy estimands, and assumption-based analyses can clarify interpretation, although they cannot identify a decomposition without additional assumptions. Existing target-trial and dynamic-strategy methods substantially address the design problem; context-specific horizon selection and adequate trajectory reporting remain unresolved practice issues. High severity is justified only when the reported estimand is mischaracterized or exposure separation is inadequate for the intended decision. Cites 10.1093/aje/kwv254: Provides the distinction between observational analogues of intention-to-treat and per-protocol effects and the corresponding design and adjustment requirements.. Cites 10.1093/ije/dyv295: Shows that later initiation and switching can be specified explicitly as dynamic strategies rather than treated as an unaddressable convergence problem..
Grok 4.5, recency lens
open / supported. Under an inverted prior, the open components of CNF-01 were treated as likely already closed by method, reporting guidance, or software; searches do not support that closure. The phenomenon itself is long recognized: observational assignment-to-initiate contrasts are confounded at baseline, and later initiation in the non-initiator arm dilutes the observational intention-to-treat (ITT) analogue as follow-up lengthens. Hernán and Hernández-Díaz (Clin Trials 2012; doi:10.1177/1740774511420743) document ITT dilution under nonadherence and argue for careful per-protocol (PP) work in comparative effectiveness. Hernán and Robins (Am J Epidemiol 2016; doi:10.1093/aje/kwv254), already cited, define observational ITT versus PP analogues and what each requires; they do not supply a reporting convention that forces cumulative initiation trajectories in both arms beside the effect estimate, nor a rule for a still-meaningful horizon. Cain et al. (Int J Epidemiol 2016; doi:10.1093/ije/dyv295), also already cited, is the main design response: name later initiation via dynamic strategies rather than an open-ended never-initiate arm. That changes the estimand; it does not identify or “debias” the attenuated ITT contrast under realistic crossover. Recent restatements keep the issue open rather than solved. Terman (Neurology 2025; doi:10.1212/WNL.0000000000214269) states that the ITT effect is typically attenuated to the degree patients switch after assignment, with a statin initiator versus noninitiator example where PP was stronger than ITT. Molero-Calafell et al. (J Clin Epidemiol 2024) restate that ITT can dilute effects when adherence differs. TARGET (Cashin et al., JAMA 2025; doi:10.1001/jama.2025.13350) is the strongest recent reporting push: it requires naming the causal contrast (assignment versus adherence), mapping strategies to data, and reporting sensitivity to design and analysis choices. From published summaries of the 21-item checklist, it does not mandate cumulative incidence of treatment initiation in both arms over follow-up as a standard diagnostic of strategy convergence, and it does not establish a horizon convention for when an initiate-now versus not-now contrast remains interpretable. STaRT-RWE, HARPER, NICE RWE framework, FDA RWE/data-source materials, ENCePP, and ICH E9(R1) improve estimand language and protocol structure generally; none close the specific CNF-01 gap that a long-horizon null observational ITT cannot be read against the convergence that produced it. Package checks (TrialEmulation, gfoRmula, lmtp, ltmle, WeightIt, MatchIt, cobalt, riskRegression, adjustedCurves, zEpid, causallib) show outcome cumulative-incidence tools and ITT/PP estimation, not a merged standard for dual-arm treatment-trajectory diagnostics or horizon-linked attenuation simulation. No 2024–2026 paper was found that (i) evaluates a field convention for publishing initiation trajectories beside the ITT estimate, (ii) defines a horizon rule for when the strategies remain distinct enough, or (iii) ships a validated simulation tool mapping observed convergence rates to expected attenuation at each horizon. Proposal and restatement are not resolution. Convergence remains a feature of the ITT strategy contrast under later initiation; PP or dynamic redesigns answer a different question. Status therefore remains open as stated. Queries run: observational ITT attenuation/dilution with later initiation and comparator crossover; target trial emulation treatment trajectories and cumulative initiation in both arms; TARGET/Cashin/Hansford reporting items on adherence and trajectories; Cain/Hernán prior art on dynamic strategies and ITT analogues; arXiv/medRxiv 2024–2026 TTE ITT crossover; CRAN/GitHub activity for TrialEmulation, gfoRmula, lmtp and related packages; FDA/EMA/NICE/ENCePP/STaRT-RWE/HARPER 2024–2026 guidance on adherence and ITT in observational emulations.
GLM-5.2, third independent lens
open / supported. The statement is internally consistent and correctly identifies a structural feature of the observational ITT analogue (convergence of strategies over time) rather than a removable bias. The reason given for the problem being open—that convergence is intrinsic to the strategy contrast, rarely reported, and lacks a convention for horizon selection—actually supports its open status. The scope is appropriate: it correctly limits the claim to the ITT analogue and acknowledges that dynamic strategies (per-protocol) are a design response, not a fix for the ITT contrast itself. The severity is proportionate because an unreported convergence can lead to a misinterpretation of a null result.

How the evidence falls in time

36 papers in the reviewed corpus bear on this problem, published 2015 to 2026. Every paper found here asserts the problem is open; none reports progress.

This problem is recurrent: three or more papers spanning at least eight years assert it independently. Sustained restatement by authors who mostly do not cite each other is the strongest evidence this reading can offer that a gap is real rather than one group’s framing.
