A cost-effectiveness analysis in the same disease put the emulated probability of at least a moderate response at 0.215 against 0.650 in the randomized trial, with a between-source odds ratio of 0.30 (95% confidence interval 0.23 to 0.37), then pooled the two into 0.336 as though the difference were sampling variation; the pooled incremental cost-effectiveness ratio came to 46,300 pounds per quality-adjusted life-year, essentially the real-world value of 46,800 rather than a number between the sources. Statement

Emulated trials are combined with randomized trials in three ways: as an added study in a network meta-analysis, as an external or hybrid control arm borrowed into a randomized trial, and as a substituted effectiveness input to an economic model. All three need a quantity that neither source supplies, the systematic difference between what the emulation estimates and what the trial estimates, and practice sets it by assumption. A bivariate network meta-analysis that bridged first-line and second-line biologic therapies in rheumatoid arthritis combined 21 randomized trials with six registry-derived target trials entered at face value, assumed equivalent to randomized data. A cost-effectiveness analysis in the same disease put the emulated probability of at least a moderate response at 0.215 against 0.650 in the randomized trial, with a between-source odds ratio of 0.30 (95% confidence interval 0.23 to 0.37), then pooled the two into 0.336 as though the difference were sampling variation; the pooled incremental cost-effectiveness ratio came to 46,300 pounds per quality-adjusted life-year, essentially the real-world value of 46,800 rather than a number between the sources. Hybrid control designs instead discount the external arm by a tuning parameter calibrated from earlier replication exercises, in which residual bias hazard ratios ran between 0.96 and 1.10 for nine of eleven emulated lung cancer control arms and between 0.66 and 1.09 for most of fifteen trials across tumor types. No criterion decides whether a given emulation is exchangeable enough to enter a network, and no borrowing method balances post-baseline treatment patterns, so a borrowed control arm can answer a different estimand from the randomized arm it augments.

Why it is open

The discrepancy is not identified. Separating it from real effect modification would require observing the same population under both designs, and the reason for combining the sources is precisely that neither covers what the other does. Cross-design synthesis machinery therefore takes the bias term as an input, whether as a design adjustment, an inflated between-study variance or a power-prior weight, all supplied by judgment rather than estimated from the data being combined. Dynamic borrowing tries to recover the weight from outcome agreement, which makes it a function of the quantity under test and works only when the sources agree for the right reason: simulation shows propensity-integrated Bayesian borrowing controlling bias and type I error under selection bias and failing under unmeasured confounding, measurement error and effect drift. The estimands also differ before any weight is applied, since a trial reports an intention-to-treat contrast under protocolized follow-up while an emulation of the same question usually reports a per-protocol contrast under routine care, and nothing aligns the two before pooling.

Probable solution or research direction

Estimate the discrepancy rather than assume it: build a bias distribution from paired emulation and trial results in the same disease area and data source, and use it as the prior on the design effect that cross-design synthesis and power-prior borrowing already require as an input. Report the pooled estimate alongside the source-specific estimates and the implied bias, so a reader can see how much of the pooled number is borrowed and from where. Require estimand alignment before pooling: state which contrast each source identifies, and reweight or re-emulate so that both answer the same question rather than pooling an efficacy estimate with an effectiveness estimate. Extend covariate balancing inside borrowing methods to post-baseline treatment patterns, treating treatment duration and subsequent therapy as time-varying variables to be weighted rather than as differences left for the borrowing parameter to absorb. The first tractable step is a prespecified discrepancy prior calibrated on the existing benchmarking literature, which turns an assumption that is currently invisible into a reported quantity.

Literature and prior-art check
open / supported-with-caveat. Searched the 1,376-record screened corpus. The headline claim holds: no record supplies the discrepancy between what an emulation estimates and what a trial estimates, and no record states a criterion for admitting an emulation into a network. The 2022 rheumatoid arthritis synthesis the entry cites is the only record in the corpus that enters emulated trials into a network with randomized trials, and it enters them at face value, so the practice the entry describes is real and unaccompanied by an admission rule. The caveat is on one sentence rather than on the entry. The entry asserts that the estimands differ before any weight is applied and that nothing aligns the two before pooling. For the external and hybrid control route that sentence is refuted by named work in the corpus. A 2024 paper crosses the five estimand-framework attributes with the seven target trial components specifically for external comparator studies and identifies the overlaps and omissions; a second 2024 paper applies both frameworks to an external control analysis in solid tumors as a worked case; a 2025 paper takes the further step of estimating per-protocol effects in external comparator analyses precisely because the intention-to-treat target is hard to interpret when adherence differs between the trial and the external arm, which is the mismatch the entry names. The alignment machinery exists for that route and is recent, so the sentence should be scoped to the network and economic-model routes, where the corpus contains nothing comparable. One scope limitation on this check: power prior, dynamic borrowing and commensurate prior together match one record out of 1,376, so the borrowing literature the entry cites lies almost entirely outside a corpus harvested on target trial phrases, and nothing here confirms or refutes the entry’s claims about it.
GPT-5.6 Sol, technical and source-code lens
open / overstated. The narrow identification problem is real: without a common-comparison anchor or defensible cross-design exchangeability restrictions, a trial-emulation difference conflates residual emulation bias, genuine effect heterogeneity, measurement differences, and sampling error. Those components are not nonparametrically separable. A discrepancy distribution learned from benchmark pairs is a transported predictive prior, not identification of bias in a new emulation; it requires a defined target class, conditional exchangeability of discrepancies, and overlap. The statement nevertheless overgeneralizes. Not every network meta-analysis, external-control analysis, or economic model requires one scalar discrepancy parameter. With overlapping comparisons or an internal randomized control, design differences or outcome commensurability can be estimated under explicit hierarchical or transport models, although their causal attribution remains assumption-dependent. Observing the same population under both designs is neither necessary, because transport assumptions may suffice, nor sufficient, because estimands, measurement, follow-up, and treatment versions may still differ. The claim that trials report intention-to-treat effects while emulations usually report per-protocol effects is categorically unsound: either design can target a treatment-policy assignment effect or a per-protocol sustained-strategy effect. Post-baseline treatment duration and subsequent therapy are mediators or intercurrent events, not baseline covariates to be generically balanced. They should remain part of a treatment-policy estimand or be placed under an explicit intervention and handled with longitudinal g-methods under sequential exchangeability and positivity for a hypothetical or per-protocol estimand. The numerical sentence is also inconsistent: probabilities 0.215 and 0.650 imply an odds ratio of 0.147, while their risk ratio is 0.331; therefore 0.30 is not the stated between-source odds ratio unless it refers to an undefined alternative contrast. Moreover, 46,300 is literally between 34,700 and 46,800, and an incremental cost-effectiveness ratio need not interpolate linearly as response probabilities change. The assertion that pooling treated all discrepancy as sampling variation also requires knowing whether the model was fixed-effect, random-effects, or hierarchical. The only explicit software claim concerns crossnma, which is absent from calibration.json and documentation/refs/packages; I therefore abstain on its capabilities and required inputs. High severity is proportionate when decision-grade pooling proceeds without estimand alignment or discrepancy sensitivity analysis, but not as a universal characterization of all cross-design synthesis.
GLM-5.2, third independent lens
abstain / supported. The claim is internally consistent and identifies a structural identification problem: the systematic difference (bias) between an emulation and a randomized trial cannot be separated from true effect modification using only the combined data, because the sources cover different populations or settings. The reason given for the problem being open (that separating discrepancy from effect modification requires observing the same population under both designs, which defeats the purpose of combining) logically entails that the discrepancy parameter is not identified from the synthesis data alone. The scope is appropriate, noting that estimands differ (ITT vs per-protocol) before any weight is applied, and that dynamic borrowing conflates the weight with the outcome agreement. The severity is proportionate, as assuming the bias term can lead to misleading pooled estimates. I abstain on the status axis because I cannot search for recent literature that might have proposed a solution to this identification problem.
