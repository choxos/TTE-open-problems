Regulators have no thresholds for adequate emulation, acceptable residual confounding or sufficient sensitivity analysis

Reporting, regulation & decision acceptance

REG-01 Confirmed open High priority Promising Tractability 3/5

Statement

FDA guidance asks sponsors to describe an approach that supports causal inference and names target trial emulation where appropriate. NICE states that some target trials cannot be adequately emulated and that bespoke data collection may then be preferable. EMA and ICH guidance emphasize fit-for-use data, prespecification and transparent non-interventional design. None supplies an acceptance criterion, and a universal numerical cutoff for emulation adequacy, residual confounding or sensitivity-analysis sufficiency would not be technically defensible if one were offered. What is missing is the defensible version: prespecified, decision-specific criteria covering protocol fidelity, identification plausibility, data fitness, diagnostics, quantitative bias analysis and robustness of the decision itself, agreed before the submission rather than argued after it. Sponsors therefore cannot know in advance whether a submission will be accepted, and reviewers have no consistent basis to accept or reject one.

Why it is open

The quantities that would be thresholded, residual confounding and estimand distance, are not identified from the data of the study in question, so any fixed cutoff would be applied to an unmeasurable object. A criterion has to be decision-specific because the tolerable bias depends on what the decision costs, which means the work is to build a family of criteria rather than a number. Agencies are also reasoning from a small and non-random set of precedents, so the empirical base for calibrating even a decision-specific criterion does not yet exist.

What has been tried

Work What it contributes
FDA guidance on non-interventional studies for drug and biological products Asks sponsors to describe an approach supporting causal inference, naming target trial emulation where appropriate, without setting an acceptance threshold.
NICE real-world evidence framework Recognizes that some target trials cannot be adequately emulated and may require bespoke data collection, which is the closest any guidance comes to a stop rule.
ICH E9(R1) addendum on estimands and sensitivity analysis in clinical trials Establishes the estimand framework and the requirement for aligned sensitivity analyses, which regulators apply to trials and have not translated into thresholds for emulations.

Probable solution or research direction

Build regulator-facing evidence dossiers that separate protocol fidelity, identification assumptions, data validation, diagnostics and decision robustness into distinct sections, so a reviewer can accept some and reject others rather than judging the whole. Develop prespecified, decision-specific acceptance criteria rather than universal cutoffs, agreed with the reviewer before the analysis. Conduct prospective qualification studies of data sources and phenotypes, and treat randomized-trial benchmarking as one input to calibration rather than the sole route to it, since the benchmarking corpus is a selected sample of emulable questions.

Verification

Verdict. Confirmed open. Auditors agreed the problem is open and no counterevidence surfaced.

The source said. “FDA guidance asks sponsors to describe an approach that supports causal inference and names target trial emulation where appropriate. NICE states that some target trials cannot be adequately emulated and that bespoke data collection may then be preferable. EMA and ICH guidance emphasize fit-for-use data, prespecification and transparent non-interventional design. What none of them supplies is a threshold: how close an emulation must be to count as adequate, how much residual confounding is acceptable for a given decision, or what set of sensitivity analyses is sufficient. Sponsors therefore cannot know in advance whether a submission will be accepted, and reviewers have no consistent basis on which to accept or reject one.”

What was wrong with it. The entry treated the absent thresholds as a gap to be filled. Universal numerical cutoffs for emulation adequacy, residual confounding and sensitivity-analysis sufficiency are not technically defensible, so their absence is partly correct rather than wholly a failure. The unmet need is prespecified, decision-specific criteria rather than universal ones, and benchmarking against randomized trials is supporting evidence rather than the only route to evaluation.

Dissent. literature dissented (supported): The corpus contains regulatory and decision-facing syntheses for oncology, cardiology devices and GLP-1 therapies, all of which describe considerations rather than thresholds. A 2026 Journal of Clinical Oncology paper addresses emulation for regulatory and clinical decision making in cancer without setting an acceptance criterion. This is consistent with the entry: guidance exists, thresholds do not, and the reason given by the entry, that the quantities that would be thresholded are not identified from the study’s own data, is a structural reason rather than an oversight.

GPT-5.6 Sol, technical and source-code lens
not-a-problem / overstated. The absence of a universal numerical threshold is not an unresolved statistical defect. Residual confounding is generally unidentified without additional information, and acceptable bias depends on the estimand, decision loss, and uncertainty. Nevertheless, lacking a universal cutoff does not leave reviewers without a principled basis, and trial benchmarking cannot uniquely validate an emulation because agreement may reflect estimand differences, sampling error, or offsetting biases. Decision-specific tolerances, quantitative bias analysis, data validation, and prespecified diagnostics provide other bases for assessment.
Grok 4.5, recency lens
open / supported. Inverted-prior search for regulatory acceptance thresholds on emulation adequacy, residual confounding, or sufficient sensitivity analysis did not find any. The problem’s own cited instruments still describe process and design expectations without numeric or decision-rule cutoffs. FDA July 2024 final guidance on EHR and claims data (and related non-interventional RWE guidance) recommends quantitative bias analysis for misclassification and reliability issues, fitness-for-use assessment, and transparent causal design including target trial emulation where appropriate, but does not state how close an emulation must be, how large residual confounding may be, or which sensitivity package is enough for acceptance. NICE’s RWE framework explicitly treats some target trials as not adequately emulable and prefers bespoke collection in those cases; secondary commentary on the final framework notes that NICE deliberately does not set minimum acceptable quality standards. EMA’s March 2025 reflection paper on RWD in non-interventional studies for regulatory purposes (EMA/99865/2025) endorses target trial emulation for causal objectives, ICH E9(R1)-aligned sensitivity analysis, and case-by-case relevance determination; it does not supply thresholds. ICH E9(R1) remains a trial estimand and sensitivity framework, not a translation into emulation acceptance rules. Adjacent methods work (E-values, tipping-point and quantitative bias analysis, Faries et al. 2025 good practices for robustness of observational evidence) equip sponsors to quantify bias under assumed parameters; that is not a regulator-issued decision threshold, and any fixed numeric bar would still be decision-dependent as the report states. No 2024–2026 FDA, EMA, HMA, ICH, NICE, Health Canada, CDA-AMC, or ENCePP instrument located here closes the gap. Queries included: FDA/NICE/EMA residual confounding threshold and acceptance criteria; quantitative bias analysis regulatory threshold; ICH E9(R1) observational thresholds; ENCePP/DARWIN acceptance criteria 2025–2026; plus review of the three cited guidance PDFs and the 2025 EMA reflection paper.
