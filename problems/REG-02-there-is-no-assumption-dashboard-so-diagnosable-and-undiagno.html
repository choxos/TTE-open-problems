There is no assumption dashboard, so diagnosable and undiagnosable assumptions are reported alike

REG-02 Partially addressed High priority Promising Tractability 5/5

Bites on: Analysis plan Cross-cutting
Seen in: Any setting

Statement

Exchangeability and consistency cannot be established from the observed data. Positivity, model behavior, balance and measurement validity can at least be diagnosed. Current reports blur the distinction: a propensity-score overlap plot is offered, and the reader is left to infer that the assumptions have been checked, when the plot bears on one of them and is silent on the rest. No standard set of diagnostics exists for an emulation, so what appears varies by author and by journal, and the absence of a diagnostic cannot be distinguished from a diagnostic that was run and not reported.

Why it is open

A dashboard is a convention rather than a discovery, and conventions require an adopting body. Several of the components are not yet standardized as measurements, since balance at each time, support by history and phenotype validity have no agreed summary statistic in the longitudinal setting. Separating the testable from the untestable also makes the untestable ones conspicuous, which is uncomfortable and is exactly the point.

What has been tried

Work What it contributes
Hansford HJ, Cashin AG, Jones MD, et al. Transparent reporting of observational studies emulating a target trial: the TARGET statement. JAMA 2025 Establishes what must be reported about the protocol and its emulation, and is explicitly not a risk-of-bias or diagnostic standard.
Hansford HJ, et al. Reporting of observational studies explicitly aiming to emulate randomized trials: a systematic review. JAMA Netw Open 2023 Across 200 published emulations, 43% did not describe all key emulation components and 57% did not describe both the target protocol and its emulation, which is the measured baseline of reporting practice.

Probable solution or research direction

Define an assumption dashboard reporting covariate balance at each time, effective sample size, weight tails, support by treatment history, censoring rates, missingness, phenotype validity and sensitivity contours, arranged so that the empirically testable diagnostics are visibly separated from the untestable assumptions. Standardize a summary statistic for longitudinal balance and for support by history, since those are the two components that currently have no agreed form. Pair the dashboard with the reporting checklist so that a missing panel is a visible omission rather than an inference.

Literature and prior-art check
open / supported. No assumption dashboard appears in the corpus. Methodological quality assessment tools for observational comparative effectiveness studies exist and score reported design features rather than requiring a standard diagnostic panel, and none separates empirically testable diagnostics from untestable assumptions in its output. The entry’s claim that a missing diagnostic cannot be distinguished from an unreported one follows from the absence of a required panel and is supported by the reporting review showing that 43% of 200 emulations did not describe all key components. Cites 10.1016/s1473-3099(26)00058-7: An appraisal instrument in this space, which scores what was reported rather than requiring a diagnostic panel.. Cites 10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2023.36023: Measures how incompletely emulations report their components, which is the baseline the entry’s claim rests on..
GPT-5.6 Sol, technical and source-code lens
open / overstated. A standardized reporting convention remains a legitimate open implementation issue, but the proposed testable versus untestable division is too coarse. Observed data cannot establish exchangeability or consistency. Empirical positivity problems, nuisance-model behavior, and measured-covariate balance can be investigated, but finite-sample diagnostics cannot establish population positivity or model correctness. Measurement validity normally requires labeled, replicated, or external validation data, and balance is a property of the fitted analysis rather than a causal assumption. A dashboard would improve transparency but would not certify identification, so this is a reporting roadmap item rather than a solution to a decision-critical methodological gap.
Grok 4.5, recency lens
open / supported. No adopted, author-facing assumption dashboard that separates diagnosable diagnostics from undiagnosable identifying assumptions for target trial emulations was found. TARGET (Hansford/Cashin et al., JAMA 2025; also BMJ co-publication) is a 21-item reporting guideline for observational studies that explicitly emulate a target trial: it requires statement of the target protocol, mapping to data, identifying assumptions, and sensitivity analyses. It is deliberately not a risk-of-bias tool and not a standardized diagnostic panel; its own positioning matches the report’s prior-work note. Hansford et al. JAMA Netw Open 2023 documents uneven reporting of emulation components, which is baseline practice, not a dashboard. ROBINS-I and ROBINS-I V2 (launch materials 2024) structure reviewer risk-of-bias judgments relative to a specified target trial; that is systematic-review assessment, not a publication convention that forces authors to display balance over time, weight tails, effective sample size, support by treatment history, phenotype validity, and sensitivity contours in a fixed layout with testable panels visually separated from exchangeability and consistency. STaRT-RWE and HARPER improve protocol prespecification and reproducibility; they do not standardize longitudinal diagnostic summaries. Software ecosystems (cobalt, WeightIt, MatchIt, ipw, gfoRmula, lmtp, ltmle, adjustedCurves) supply balance, overlap, and weight diagnostics for specific designs, including some longitudinal use, but there is no field-wide agreed summary statistic for longitudinal balance or support-by-history, and no journal or regulator-mandated dashboard that makes omission of a panel a visible reporting failure. Method papers that propose or apply QBA, negative controls, or multi-panel diagnostics remain proposals or local practice, not an adopting-body convention. The core structural claim remains: conventions require adoption, and none has closed the reported gap. Queries included: assumption/diagnostic dashboard target trial; TARGET as diagnostic standard; longitudinal balance standardization cobalt WeightIt; ROBINS-I TTE diagnostics; STaRT-RWE HARPER assumption reporting 2024–2026; arXiv/medRxiv causal diagnostic checklist.

How the evidence falls in time

109 papers in the reviewed corpus bear on this problem, published 2006 to 2026. Partial progress is on the record, and later work still calls this open. Both belong in view.

This problem is recurrent: three or more papers spanning at least eight years assert it independently. Sustained restatement by authors who mostly do not cite each other is the strongest evidence this reading can offer that a gap is real rather than one group’s framing.

It is also being closed in pieces: partial results come from more than one paper in more than one year, each covering a different part.
