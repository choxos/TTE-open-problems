STR-01 Unverifiable High priority Emerging Tractability 3/5

Statement

Estimating a per-protocol effect requires the sustained strategy and its relevant treatment versions to be clinically specified first; artificial censoring with inverse probability weighting is one valid estimation route rather than a requirement. Beneath the mechanics sits the question the mechanics assume answered: what counts as the same treatment. Real treatments differ by dose, route, provider, formulation, combination and adherence pattern, and two regimens sharing a label may not satisfy consistency. Clone-censor-weight handles a patient compatible with several predefined strategies and does not decide whether versions should be pooled into one. Observed data can expose heterogeneity across versions and cannot establish that versions are irrelevant, so the pooling decision is clinical. Finer definitions also create practical support problems, so the more faithful specification is often the less identifiable one, and no guidance exists for making that trade.

Why it is open

Consistency is an assumption about the world rather than about the data, so no amount of data adjudicates whether two versions are the same treatment; heterogeneity across versions is evidence against pooling and its absence is not evidence for it. The clinical judgment that would adjudicate is specific to the drug, the indication and the era, which makes a general criterion the wrong shape. And making the versions explicit multiplies the strategies, each costing positivity, so the honest specification is frequently the one the data cannot support.

Probable solution or research direction

Use hierarchical treatment ontologies and latent treatment-version models so the equivalence class is an explicit, versioned object, and report the observed heterogeneity across versions as evidence bearing on the pooling decision rather than as a robustness check. Estimate joint effects of initiation, dose and adherence through longitudinal multivariate modified treatment policies where an analyst-specified joint policy is what is wanted, noting that such an estimator does not supply the equivalence classes and need not target the same per-protocol estimand. Quantify by simulation how much bias a wrong equivalence class induces relative to the positivity cost of the correct one, since that trade is the actual decision.

Verification

Verdict. Unverifiable. Auditors disagreed and the disagreement was evidence-bearing on both sides.

The source said. “A per-protocol effect requires censoring at deviation and weighting for the informative censoring that censoring creates. Beneath that mechanics sits a question the mechanics assume away: what counts as the same treatment. Real treatments differ by dose, route, provider, formulation, combination and adherence pattern, and two regimens sharing a label may not satisfy consistency. Clone-censor-weight handles the case where baseline assignment is genuinely ambiguous, at the cost of data expansion, artificial censoring and sensitivity to the censoring model, but it takes the strategy definition as given. There is no guidance for constructing defensible equivalence classes of treatment versions, and none for overlapping combination therapies where a patient is simultaneously compatible with more than one labeled strategy.”

What was wrong with it. Three corrections. Artificial censoring with inverse probability weighting is one valid way to estimate a per-protocol effect, not a requirement. Clone-censor-weight does handle individuals compatible with several predefined strategies, which the entry listed as unaddressed; what it does not do is decide whether treatment versions should be pooled. And observed data can expose heterogeneity across versions even though they cannot establish that versions are irrelevant, so the situation is not entirely uninformative.

Dissent. literature dissented (supported-with-caveat): The treatment-versions problem has a longer literature than the entry credits. Confounding adjustment in the presence of multiple versions of treatment was worked out for an organ-transplant application in 2019, and marginal structural models for partial exposure regimes date to 2008. A 2026 iScience paper shows that changing the exposure definition unmasks confounding in an applied emulation, which is empirical evidence for the entry’s premise and also evidence that the field is examining it. What remains genuinely absent is guidance for constructing defensible equivalence classes and for overlapping combination therapies, which is the constructive half of the entry rather than the diagnostic half. codex dissented (overstated): Treatment-version ambiguity is real: collapsing causally different doses, formulations, routes, combinations, or adherence patterns can make the named intervention ill-defined. However, the report overstates both the required mechanics and the remaining gap. Estimating a per-protocol effect does not require artificial censoring plus inverse-probability weighting; the longitudinal g-formula and targeted or sequential-regression estimators can estimate sustained-strategy effects under their respective identification and modeling conditions. Clone-censor-weight already provides the bookkeeping for individuals compatible with multiple prespecified strategies, although it cannot choose those strategies or determine which versions are equivalent. Observed data cannot prove treatment-version irrelevance, but version-specific data can reveal heterogeneity and inform clinical judgment. Finer definitions may worsen practical support and precision, but every additional strategy does not mechanically violate positivity. Pinned lmtp 1.5.4 is correctly described: its NAMESPACE exports lmtp_tmle and lmtp_sdr, while its source accepts list-valued treatment nodes and applies analyst-specified multivariate shifts. It therefore supports estimation after a joint policy has been defined, not construction of clinical equivalence classes. Moreover, a longitudinal modified treatment policy generally targets a natural-value-dependent policy estimand, not automatically the per-protocol effect of a fixed sustained strategy. The issue is decision-critical when collapsed versions have materially different effects; a universal treatment ontology or latent-version model is a roadmap item rather than a prerequisite for every well-specified emulation.

Grok 4.5, recency lens
open / supported. Inverted-prior search does not find a procedure that constructs clinically defensible equivalence classes of treatment versions for consistency in sustained-strategy target-trial emulation. The report’s own cited works do not close the gap: Gaber et al. (2024, 10.1002/cam4.70461) is a didactic CCW primer that takes the strategy as given; Cain et al. (2016, 10.1093/ije/dyv295) is the closest specification template for dynamic switching but does not adjudicate dose, route, formulation, provider, or combination-version equivalence; lmtp supplies estimation for continuous and multivariate modified treatment policies once an intervention is defined, not a clinical rule for which versions may be labeled the same treatment. Classic prior art formalizes the identification problem without solving clinical construction: VanderWeele and Hernán (2013, J Causal Inference; multiple versions) and Hernán and VanderWeele (2011, compound treatments) show when version-mixed averages versus version-specific effects are identifiable, which strengthens rather than resolves the claim that consistency is a world assumption adjudicated by clinical judgment specific to drug, indication, and era. 2024–2026 work remains proposal-level or off-scope. Yoshikawa and Kawano (arXiv:2601.00287, 2026) propose mixture-of-experts estimation of latent versions under point treatment; the number of versions is pre-specified, versions are data-driven latent classes rather than clinically specified equivalence classes, and the paper itself notes that version-specific methodology was previously underdeveloped. That is a partial advance along the report’s proposed latent-version direction, not a solved clinical procedure, and it does not cover sustained or dynamic strategies or overlapping multi-drug regimens. Ostropolets et al. (2024) ATC–RxNorm hierarchies improve drug coding and retrieval; they are not causal equivalence classes for consistency. Fu et al. (BMJ 2026) and related CCW guidance clarify cloning when baseline data are compatible with more than one labeled strategy; that is the mechanics the report already credits, still leaving strategy definition as input. Practical TTE guides (e.g. Fridén 2026) restate “define interventions so relevant multiple versions do not exist” without a constructive algorithm. Package release notes and GitHub activity for TrialEmulation, gfoRmula, gfoRmulaICE, lmtp, ltmle, WeightIt, MatchIt, and related tools implement estimation given a user-defined regimen; none encode an equivalence-class constructor. 2025–2026 FDA RWE / externally controlled trial materials, EMA/HMA registry guidance, HARPER/STaRT-RWE, ENCePP, and TARGET-style reporting require explicit treatment-strategy specification but do not supply a general method for building clinically defensible version sets or for adjudicating simultaneous multi-strategy compatibility beyond cloning. Because consistency is structurally untestable from data alone, sensitivity analyses and latent-mixture recoveries quantify consequences or statistical heterogeneity; they do not identify clinical sameness. Queries that returned nothing closing the gap included combinations of: treatment version equivalence class + target trial; clinically defensible versions of treatment + consistency; hierarchical ontology + causal strategy definition + sustained treatment; overlapping combination therapy + clone-censor equivalence; and journal-restricted 2024–2026 searches in AJE, Epidemiology, IJE, Stat Med, Biometrics, BMJ, JAMA, plus arXiv stat.ME and medRxiv. Residual risk is a narrowly scoped clinical protocol for one drug class that was not indexed under causal-inference terms; that would at most partially narrow the problem for that class, not the general gap as stated.
glm
abstain / supported-with-caveat. The claim is internally consistent and identifies a genuine structural tension: consistency is an assumption about the world, not the data, so clinical adjudication of treatment-version equivalence is inherently context-specific (drug, indication, era). The report’s own reasoning—that ‘a general criterion [is] the wrong shape for the problem’—supports a structural impossibility for a universal procedure, which is a stronger and more defensible claim than the empirical assertion that ‘no guidance exists.’ The latter is a negative existential about the current literature that I cannot verify without search. Additionally, the report itself cites Cain et al. 2016 as ‘the closest existing template for specifying what a strategy includes,’ which suggests related guidance exists even if it does not directly address equivalence-class construction. The claim is pitched at a level that conflates a structural limitation (no general procedure can work) with an empirical gap (no guidance has been written), and the proposed direction (ontologies, latent models, modified treatment policies) implies that partial frameworks are possible, undercutting the implication that the problem is permanently intractable. The severity is proportionate: a wrong equivalence class induces bias, and the positivity trade-off is a real decision cost. I abstain on status because confirming the absence of all guidance requires literature search I cannot perform.
