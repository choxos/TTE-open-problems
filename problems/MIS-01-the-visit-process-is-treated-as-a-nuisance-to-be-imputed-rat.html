Statement

Biomarkers and diagnoses are observed when a patient attends care and a clinician orders a test. That visit process depends on latent severity, on prior treatment and often on an impending outcome, so record-based covariate histories are irregular and selectively missing in a way that carries clinical information. Missing-at-random conditional on the recorded history can therefore fail precisely because the recording is itself informative. Standard handling, and last observation carried forward in particular, constructs an artificial history in which a patient’s state is asserted to be unchanged over an interval during which the absence of measurement was itself evidence about their state.

Why it is open

Conditioning on having been observed can open a collider path, so the natural fix makes a different bias, and choosing between them requires knowing the structure that is in question. Inverse-intensity-of-visit weights are unstable when visit rates vary by an order of magnitude across patients, which they do. Modeling the visit process jointly with treatment and outcome multiplies the nuisance components and therefore the positivity requirements, so the more faithful model can be the less identifiable one.

What has been tried

Work What it contributes

Wang, et al. An operational target trial emulation framework for causal inference using electronic health records. npj Digit Med 2026 Places the observation process among the operational constraints an emulation must handle rather than among its data-cleaning steps.

Probable solution or research direction

Model visits as recurrent events or point processes jointly with treatment and outcome, and compare complete case, last observation carried forward, multiple imputation, inverse-intensity weighting and joint latent-state targeted learning on data generated with a known, treatment-dependent visiting mechanism. Include healthcare use as a process in the causal diagram rather than as a baseline confounder, so that conditioning decisions can be read off the graph. Report visit-weight dispersion and the calibration of the predicted visit intensity, since an uncalibrated intensity model produces weights that look like adjustment and are noise.

Literature and prior-art check
partially-solved / overstated. The entry says standard handling constructs artificial histories and that modeling visits jointly with treatment and outcome multiplies nuisance components without a worked solution. Three separate estimator families in the corpus contradict the strong reading. A 2024 Biometrics paper proposes a multiply robust estimator for marginal structural models under irregularly spaced, covariate-driven observation times and shows it is more flexible and more efficient than the previously proposed doubly weighted estimator. A 2023 BMC Medical Research Methodology paper gives calibrated inverse probability weights for marginal structural models under irregular visits with an unmeasured confounder. A 2025 Statistics in Medicine paper builds continuous-time weights from a marked point process for the same reason. What survives is an adoption claim rather than a methods claim: these estimators exist and applied emulation pipelines do not use them, and healthcare use is still entered as a baseline confounder rather than modeled as a process. Cites 10.1093/biomtc/ujae065: Supplies a multiply robust estimator for exactly the setting the entry describes, and demonstrates it is more efficient than the existing doubly weighted alternative.. Cites 10.1186/s12874-022-01831-2: Gives calibrated weights for irregular visits with an unmeasured confounder, addressing the weight instability the entry raises.. Cites 10.1002/sim.70102: Models visits as a point process jointly with treatment, which is the direction the entry proposes..
GPT-5.6 Sol, technical and source-code lens
open / overstated. The central problem is technically real. When visit or test ordering depends on treatment and on an unobserved current prognostic state, missingness need not be at random given recorded history; LOCF then replaces an unknown trajectory with a constant one. Identification remains open without additional structural assumptions, external measurements, or sensitivity parameters. Several supporting claims are too broad. Conditioning on observation opens a collider path only under particular causal structures. Inverse-intensity weights require conditional independent observation, correct intensity models, and observation positivity; heterogeneity in marginal visit rates, even by an order of magnitude, does not by itself make the weights unstable. Joint modeling adds nuisance models and identifying assumptions, but it does not automatically multiply positivity requirements. Calibration is useful but insufficient because a conditionally misspecified intensity model can be marginally calibrated. Very high severity is proportionate only when decision-relevant eligibility variables, confounders, or outcomes are materially visit-dependent, not for every record-based emulation.
Grok 4.5, recency lens
partially-solved / supported-with-caveat. Inverted-prior search finds methods that put the visit process in the causal or estimating structure, so the problem is not open as a pure absence of methodology. It is not fully solved either: the report’s hard tradeoffs remain live, and the comparative evaluation it proposes has not been delivered for sustained-strategy target trial emulation under a known, treatment-dependent visiting mechanism.

Cited works first. Goldstein et al. (JAMIA 2019; 10.1093/jamia/ocz148) characterize when informative visits bias EHR association analyses; that paper diagnoses the mechanism and does not close it. Wang et al. (npj Digit Med 2026; 10.1038/s41746-026-02563-z) elevate the observation process to an operational constraint on whether an EHR can realize a target trial protocol element. Secondary commentary on that paper treats the contribution as specification-versus-realization discipline, not as a solved estimator. Both strengthen the claim that the issue is still on the agenda; neither resolves identification or estimation under treatment-dependent visiting.

Prior art that treats visits as structure rather than pure nuisance: (i) inverse-intensity-of-visit (IIW) theory and software for irregular longitudinal data (Pullenayegum line; CRAN IrregLong with Cox visit-intensity weights and IIW-GEE), still actively refined in 2025 on visit-model variable selection and selection of patients with no follow-up; (ii) parametric g-formula software that models a random measurement or visit process jointly with covariates for sustained strategies (gfoRmula’s visitprocess argument: visit indicators attached to intermittently measured confounders, with last-measured values and a cap on consecutive missed visits); (iii) Kalia et al. (BMC Med Res Methodol 2023; 10.1186/s12874-022-01831-2), calibrated inverse probability weights for marginal structural models under irregular visits, with Monte Carlo evaluation; (iv) Kalia et al. (Stat Med 2025; 10.1002/sim.70102), continuous-time MSM weights factored as visit intensity times dose (marked point process) on primary-care EHR for cumulative SGLT-2i exposure and recurrent UTI, explicitly motivated by informative visit timing. Coulombe et al. (Stat Methods Med Res 2023) combine inverse intensity of visit weights with individualized treatment rules in longitudinal observational data, which is related but not full sustained-strategy TTE.

What remains open, and why a full solved vote fails. IIW and continuous-time visit weights still require sequential positivity for the visit intensity and can be unstable when intensities vary sharply across patients; Kalia 2025 itself discusses extreme weights, stabilization, and constrained optimal weights, not elimination of the variance problem the report flags. Joint visit–treatment–outcome models still multiply nuisance components and positivity requirements; that structural cost is not removed by packaging. gfoRmula’s visit process assumes a discrete grid and a correctly specified visit model, and still encodes non-visit intervals via last measured values, so it does not by itself refute the LOCF critique for continuous-time EHR generation. No 2024–2026 paper or package release was found that runs the report’s proposed horse race (complete case, LOCF, MI, IIW, joint latent-state targeted learning) on data generated with a known treatment-dependent visiting mechanism in a sustained-strategy TTE design. Method proposals and single-setting applications therefore count as partial, not full, resolution.

Package and guidance scan. IrregLong and gfoRmula implement visit or measurement process handling; TrialEmulation, lmtp, ltmle, WeightIt, MatchIt, cobalt, ipw, adjustedCurves, riskRegression, zEpid, causallib, lifelines, and scikit-survival did not surface merged work that closes this gap for informative EHR visit processes under sustained strategies. 2025–2026 RWE guidances (FDA RWE, EMA/HMA registry, ICH E9(R1), TARGET, HARPER/STaRT-RWE, ENCePP, ISPE, NICE) frame data-generation and missingness issues at a high level; none was found that settles a preferred causal handling of informative visits for TTE.

Caveat on the claim as stated. Applied TTE and EHR pipelines still often grid time and carry forward or impute under MAR, so the nuisance framing is still a fair description of common practice. The methodological literature has long refused that framing, so a reader who only knows IIW, gfoRmula visitprocess, or continuous-time marked point process MSMs would find the absolute statement overstated. The agenda item that remains is comparative, practical, and identification-theoretic under realistic visit heterogeneity, not the invention of the first joint visit model.

GLM-5.2, third independent lens
open / supported. The problem statement accurately describes a methodological tension in EHR-based target trial emulation: the visit process is informative, yet standard practice treats it as a nuisance to be imputed. The reasons given for it being open (collider bias from conditioning on observation, instability of inverse-intensity weights, and positivity violations from joint modeling) are valid structural tradeoffs that genuinely support the claim being open. The claim is proportionate and does not assert an impossibility, but rather highlights a difficult modeling tradeoff that remains unresolved in standard practice.

How the evidence falls in time

93 papers in the reviewed corpus bear on this problem, published 2010 to 2026. Progress and a fresh assertion of openness land in the same year.

This problem is recurrent: three or more papers spanning at least eight years assert it independently. Sustained restatement by authors who mostly do not cite each other is the strongest evidence this reading can offer that a gap is real rather than one group’s framing.

It is also being closed in pieces: partial results come from more than one paper in more than one year, each covering a different part.
