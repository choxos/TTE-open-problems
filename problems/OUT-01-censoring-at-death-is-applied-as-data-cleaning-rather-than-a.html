Statement

Death, treatment discontinuation and competing clinical events change what an outcome contrast means. ICH E9(R1) requires that intercurrent events be handled explicitly, and the causal competing-risks literature distinguishes total effects, controlled direct effects and separable effects, each answering a different question. Applied emulations nonetheless default to cause-specific hazards or censor competing events without matching the estimator to the causal question, and recurrent and terminal events are seldom modeled jointly. The step where an analyst censors follow-up at death is an identifying decision that selects among these estimands, and it is normally executed silently inside data preparation, where no reviewer sees it as a decision at all.

Why it is open

The estimands differ in what they assume rather than in what they fit, so a wrong choice produces a well-behaved analysis of the wrong quantity and nothing downstream flags it. Separable effects require decomposing a treatment into components whose separate effects are meaningful, which is a substantive claim about the drug that most applied settings cannot support. Recurrent-event and terminal-event models are available but their combination with sustained-strategy emulation is not worked out, so the analyst choosing correctly on competing risks may have no estimator for the regime they wanted.

What has been tried

Work What it contributes
ICH E9(R1) addendum on estimands and sensitivity analysis in clinical trials Requires explicit specification of the strategy for each intercurrent event, which is the discipline that the emulation literature has not yet inherited.
lmtp: Non-Parametric Causal Effects of Feasible Interventions Based on Modified Treatment Policies. CRAN Supports censored outcomes and competing risks for modified treatment policies, one of the few implementations where the competing event is part of the estimand rather than a preprocessing step.

Probable solution or research direction

Prespecify the estimand as a cumulative incidence, a restricted mean event-free time or a recurrent-event contrast, and name the intercurrent-event strategy for each competing event before the analysis. Compare multistate g-computation, joint frailty models, separable effects and recurrent-event targeted learning on data generated with a known competing-event structure, so the cost of the default is measured rather than asserted. Require that any censoring at a competing event be justified in the protocol as an identifying choice, with the estimand it implies stated, which turns a data-cleaning line into a reviewable claim.

Literature and prior-art check
open / supported-with-caveat. The methodological apparatus the entry asks for exists further than it allows. A 2026 Journal of Epidemiology paper treats causal inference from time-to-event data with dependent censoring, confounding and competing events together, and a 2025 Journal of Clinical Epidemiology paper works a trial emulation with competing risks in a surgical application. The entry’s claim is nonetheless about practice rather than method: that applied emulations default to cause-specific hazards or censor at competing events without matching the estimator to the question, and that censoring at death is executed inside data preparation. Nothing in the corpus contradicts that, and the reporting requirement the entry proposes does not exist. GPT-5.6 Sol, technical and source-code lens
partially-solved / overstated. The central warning is valid and potentially high severity: handling death or another intercurrent event is part of defining the estimand, and total, controlled direct, and separable effects answer different questions. The entry is nevertheless conceptually imprecise. An estimand is defined by its counterfactual outcomes and contrast; identification assumptions are separate. Simply censoring at death does not select or identify one of those causal effects. Depending on the analysis, it can instead produce a cause-specific risk-set quantity or an unidentified hypothetical contrast. The claims that censoring is normally silent and that recurrent and terminal events are seldom modeled jointly are empirical frequency claims not established by the cited package source. Pinned lmtp source exports lmtp_tmle, lmtp_sdr, and lmtp_survival; accepts longitudinal treatment, censoring, and competing-risk nodes; incorporates competing-event status in its recursion; and tests two treatment times with two competing-risk times. Pinned gfoRmula source exports gformula_survival and static and implements sustained intervention risks with an explicit competing-event model and an option governing whether that event is treated as censoring. These implementations address competing risks under longitudinal strategies, but neither exposes an explicit joint recurrent-event and terminal-event estimand. The narrower implementation and protocol gap therefore remains. Cites CRAN:lmtp@1.5.4: The pinned NAMESPACE exports lmtp_tmle, lmtp_sdr, and lmtp_survival. The source accepts treatment variables ordered over time together with censoring and competing-risk indicators, and its fidelity test applies repeated treatment and competing-risk nodes. This supplies estimators for cumulative incidence under longitudinal treatment policies, but not an explicit joint recurrent-terminal contrast.. Cites CRAN:gfoRmula@1.1.1: The pinned NAMESPACE exports gformula_survival and static. The source models competing events and implements never-treat and always-treat interventions across follow-up, resolving ordinary sustained-strategy competing-risk estimation while leaving explicit recurrent-terminal estimands unaddressed..
Grok 4.5, recency lens
partially-solved / supported-with-caveat. Inverted-prior search shows the methodological core of OUT-01 is older and better equipped than a pure ‘open’ label implies, while the applied silence problem remains. Young, Stensrud, Tchetgen Tchetgen and Hernán (2020) already formalize that treating a competing event as censoring selects a controlled-direct-effect style question rather than a total-effect cumulative incidence, so the ‘identifying assumption vs data cleaning’ distinction is settled in the causal competing-risks literature. Software already embeds that choice for sustained or feasible longitudinal interventions: gfoRmula documents explicit options for competing events under time-varying treatment strategies, and lmtp implements time-to-event outcomes with competing risks for modified treatment policies, including a later ‘total effect’ option in version 1.5.0 (citing Díaz, Hoffman and Hejazi). Janvin, Young, Ryalen and Stensrud (2024) formalize causal estimands for recurrent events with and without a terminal competing event. None of that fully closes OUT-01 as stated. Applied primers still describe censor-at-death plus Fine-Gray sensitivity as common practice (Terman et al., 2025). ICH E9(R1) still governs trials more tightly than routine emulation protocols; TARGET (2025) improves TTE reporting generally but does not by itself force an intercurrent-event strategy for death the way E9(R1) does for trials. Separable effects remain a substantive product claim, not a software switch. Joint recurrent-plus-terminal estimation under longitudinal confounding is advancing (Prosepe et al., 2025 multistate g-computation; D’Alessio et al., 2026 arXiv g-computation for recurrent and semi-competing events with time-varying actions) but those are method papers and proposals, not field-wide defaults or head-to-head evaluations of multistate g-computation, joint frailty, separable effects and recurrent-event targeted learning on a known competing-event DGP. Net: estimator and formal estimand machinery for competing death under many sustained strategies exist; silent preprocessing and incomplete recurrent/terminal tooling keep the problem only partially solved. GLM-5.2, third independent lens
open / supported. The statement is internally consistent and proportionate. It accurately characterizes a well-known methodological tension in causal inference and target trial emulation: handling competing events (like death) via censoring during data preparation implicitly imposes an identifying assumption and selects an estimand, often without explicit protocol-level justification. The reason given for the problem being open—that estimands differ in their assumptions and a mismatched choice yields a well-behaved but wrong analysis without downstream flags—logically supports the assertion that this remains an open issue in applied practice. The scope is appropriate, addressing current applied practice rather than asserting a structural impossibility.

How the evidence falls in time

53 papers in the reviewed corpus bear on this problem, published 2017 to 2026. Progress and a fresh assertion of openness land in the same year.

This problem is recurrent: three or more papers spanning at least eight years assert it independently. Sustained restatement by authors who mostly do not cite each other is the strongest evidence this reading can offer that a gap is real rather than one group’s framing.

It is also being closed in pieces: partial results come from more than one paper in more than one year, each covering a different part.
