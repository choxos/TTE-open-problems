Positivity diagnostics describe weights instead of naming which patient histories are unsupported

Positivity, structural violations & extrapolation
Very high priority
Partially addressed
A deterministic treatment strategy can assign a treatment that clinicians almost never give to patients with certain covariate histories. The standard response is to inspect the weight distribution and truncate the tail. Truncation is bias-variance regularization: it cannot identify an effect where the treatment density is structurally zero, and a marginal weight summary does not say which intervention-relevant histories carry small or zero density. What a reader needs and does not get is a policy-specific support criterion fixed in advance, diagnostics localized by time and by history, the fraction of the target population that fails the criterion, and the estimand that survives once those strata are set aside. OVL-01 Partially addressed Very high priority Promising Tractability 4/5

Statement

A deterministic treatment strategy can assign a treatment that clinicians almost never give to patients with certain covariate histories. The standard response is to inspect the weight distribution and truncate the tail. Truncation is bias-variance regularization: it cannot identify an effect where the treatment density is structurally zero, and a marginal weight summary does not say which intervention-relevant histories carry small or zero density. What a reader needs and does not get is a policy-specific support criterion fixed in advance, diagnostics localized by time and by history, the fraction of the target population that fails the criterion, and the estimand that survives once those strata are set aside. Empirically, poor overlap has been shown to defeat adjustment methods that were otherwise well specified, which is the failure a weight histogram is least able to show. Software for stochastic and modified treatment policies covers part of the continuous longitudinal case; partial-identification bounds for arbitrary sustained strategies do not exist.

Why it is open

Practical positivity is a property of a conditional density over a growing history, so beyond a few time points the object to be diagnosed is high-dimensional and cannot be plotted. Truncation is attractive precisely because it converts an identification failure into a variance reduction, which reads as a modeling improvement rather than as a change of question. Reporting an unsupported fraction also requires deciding what counts as support, and any such criterion is a decision about the estimand that nobody is currently required to state.

What has been tried

Work What it contributes
Target trial emulation under nonmutually exclusive assignment. Am J Epidemiol 2026;195(4):1045 Shows that poor overlap can defeat advanced adjustment methods, in a setting where assignment to strategies is not mutually exclusive.
lmtp: Non-Parametric Causal Effects of Feasible Interventions Based on Modified Treatment Policies. CRAN Implements modified treatment policies and stochastic interventions for binary, categorical, continuous and multivariate treatments with censored outcomes and competing risks, which is the estimand class that restores positivity.

Probable solution or research direction

Prespecify a policy-specific support criterion, report time- and history-localized support diagnostics against it, and report the sensitivity of the conclusion to the criterion, so the definition is visible rather than implicit. Replace or restrict deterministic regimes the data cannot support with modified treatment policies, incremental propensity-score interventions or stochastic initiation rules where that is scientifically acceptable, and say so in the paper rather than in the truncation. Derive partial-identification bounds for the unsupported strata under sustained strategies, which is the piece no current software supplies.

Verification

Verdict. Partially addressed. Part of this problem is addressed by existing work; the entry names which part.

The source said. “A deterministic treatment strategy can assign a treatment that clinicians almost never give to patients with certain covariate histories. The standard response is to inspect the weight distribution and truncate the tail, which reduces variance, changes the implicit estimand and can leave substantial bias in place. What the diagnostic does not report is the quantity a reader needs: what fraction of the target population has no support for the strategy under study, which histories are the unsupported ones, and what estimand remains identified once they are set aside. A 2026 study of non-mutually exclusive assignment found that poor overlap defeated adjustment methods that were otherwise well specified, which is the failure mode a weight histogram is least able to show.”

What was wrong with it. Truncation is bias-variance regularization and does not change the estimand in the way the entry implied; what it cannot do is identify an effect under structural nonpositivity. Software for stochastic and modified treatment policies also partly addresses the continuous longitudinal case, so the alternatives the entry proposes are not entirely unavailable.

Dissent. literature dissented (open): The entry’s core claim, that diagnostics describe weights rather than naming which histories are unsupported, holds against the corpus. Two pieces of work sharpen rather than resolve it. A 2025 study measures the impact of near-positivity violations on IPTW-estimated marginal structural survival models with time-dependent covariates, which supplies the consequence the entry asserts. A 2026 American Journal of Epidemiology paper on target trial emulation under non-mutually exclusive assignment sets out structural pitfalls and remedies for exactly the overlap failure the entry cites. Neither produces a causal support map over histories, an unsupported-fraction statistic, or partial-identification bounds for the unsupported strata, so the specific artifacts the entry says are missing are still missing. codex dissented (overstated): A marginal weight histogram cannot localize poor support to particular histories, and structural nonpositivity is a decision-grade identification failure. However, the report conflates structural nonpositivity, practical near-nonpositivity, and finite-sample instability. Weight truncation does not convert an identification failure into variance reduction or necessarily define a coherent alternative causal estimand. It changes the estimating functional and usually biases estimation of the stated target, while structural nonpositivity remains unidentified. The claim that the high-dimensional object cannot be plotted is also too absolute because reduced history-specific diagnostics are possible. Pinned CICI 1.0 is a direct partial counterexample: its NAMESPACE exports feasible, plot.feasible, and summary.feasible; feasible() estimates conditional treatment densities, returns per-observation feasible intervention values and low_matrix objects, and reports the proportion below its threshold by strategy and time. Its gformula accepts the resulting individual intervention matrices. This implementation is limited to continuous interventions and an analyst-chosen alpha threshold, does not provide general partial-identification bounds, and does not cover arbitrary binary or categorical sustained strategies. Its threshold implementation also normalizes densities using bin widths but calculates cumsum(sort(row)) without bin-width weighting, so the advertised probability-mass threshold is miscalibrated whenever bin widths differ from one. Later-time diagnostics are evaluated over observed histories, which need not equal the history distribution induced by the intervention. Pinned lmtp 1.5.4 implements longitudinal modified treatment policies and an ipsi policy factory, but such intervention classes do not automatically restore positivity; policy-specific absolute continuity remains necessary, and lmtp trims estimated density ratios by default. Very-high severity is warranted when the prespecified strategy materially lacks support, but a universal support map and bounds are roadmap extensions rather than requirements for every adequately supported analysis.

Grok 4.5, recency lens
partially-solved / supported-with-caveat. Inverted prior: treat the diagnostic gap as already closed, then check. The report’s own citations do not close it. Takayama (AJE 2026; 10.1093/aje/kwaf018) shows that poor overlap can defeat well-specified adjustment under non-mutually exclusive assignment; that is evidence the failure mode is real, not a fix for naming unsupported histories. lmtp implements modified treatment policies (MTPs) and stochastic interventions that can restore positivity by changing the estimand; that is the estimand-class response the agenda already proposes, not a diagnostic that reports which histories lack support or what fraction of the target population is unsupported.

What does close most of the stated gap is recent prior art the agenda does not cite. Danelian et al. (Journal of Causal Inference 2023; 10.1515/jci-2022-0032) introduced PoRT, a regression-tree diagnostic that flags in-sample positivity violations as interpretable covariate-defined subgroups, with implementation in the CRAN package RISCA (port). Chatton et al. (Epidemiology 2025; 10.1097/EDE.0000000000001902) extend this to longitudinal static and dynamic strategies as sPoRT, explicitly motivated by the inadequacy of inspecting cumulative inverse-probability weights: weight products can flag sparsity under correctly specified propensity models but do not name the problematic subgroups. In their HIV ART reanalysis, sPoRT returns time-updated subgroups with size as a share of those who should follow the rule (e.g., a CD4/WAZ-defined cell of n*=43, 5.4% of those assigned to initiate at 6 months) and recommended responses include redesign of the rule, exclusion of structurally unsupported baseline strata as a design decision, and switch to alternative estimands. Ring and Schomaker (Journal of Causal Inference 2026; 10.1515/jci-2025-0007; arXiv:2502.11820) add a kernel-based, estimand-specific sparsity diagnostic for continuous treatments and MTPs that reports effective data points per intervened observation, supports comparing alternative estimands before estimation, and is tied to the CICI / lmtp ecosystem. Moodie and Schulz (Statistics in Medicine 2025; 10.1002/sim.70194) give a hat-value style diagnostic for continuous exposures. Together these tools deliver the three quantities the problem says weight histograms do not: unsupported fraction (via subgroup size or near-zero EDP mass), which histories fail (tree leaves or low-EDP units), and guidance on the estimand that remains identifiable (rule redesign, population restriction, or MTP choice).

What remains open, so a full “solved” vote would be wrong: (1) no agreed support threshold (α, β in PoRT/sPoRT; kernel bandwidth in Ring) is still a free estimand decision; (2) partial-identification bounds for the unsupported strata are not a packaged, routine output of these diagnostics; (3) the methods are not first-class diagnostics inside the main TTE stacks the agenda names (TrialEmulation, gfoRmula, lmtp, WeightIt/cobalt still center weight and balance summaries rather than history-level support maps); (4) very high-dimensional continuous longitudinal histories remain hard to plot as a single “causal support map,” which is a packaging and reporting problem more than an identification one. Searches that returned no stronger closer: TTE-package release notes / PRs for TrialEmulation, gfoRmula, gfoRmulaICE, lmtp, WeightIt, MatchIt, cobalt, CICI for a history-level support map; TARGET / HARPER / STaRT-RWE / ENCePP / FDA-RWE 2025–2026 guidance mandating unsupported-fraction reporting; arXiv/medRxiv 2024–2026 for partial-ID bounds specifically for positivity-unsupported TTE strata as standard practice. Bottom line: the claim that standard weight diagnostics hide the real object is still right for modal practice, but the claim that the field has no method that names unsupported histories is no longer true after PoRT (2023), sPoRT (2025), and Ring–Schomaker (2026). Cites 10.1515/jci-2022-0032: Point-treatment precursor: nonparametric regression-tree search for sparse propensity cells defined by covariate values, without requiring a correctly specified parametric propensity model; shipped as port in CRAN RISCA.. glm
open / supported. The problem statement accurately identifies a gap in current positivity diagnostics: they focus on weight distributions rather than identifying unsupported patient histories and the resulting estimand. The reasons given for it being open (high-dimensionality of histories, lack of agreed thresholds for support) are valid and support the claim that this is an open methodological challenge. The claim is proportionate and does not overstate the issue as an impossibility where it is merely a current practice gap, though it correctly notes the structural difficulty of plotting high-dimensional objects.
