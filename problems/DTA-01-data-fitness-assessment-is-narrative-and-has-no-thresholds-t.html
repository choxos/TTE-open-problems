DTA-01 Overstated Very high priority Emerging Tractability 4/5

Statement

Electronic health records arise from encounters, billing and documentation rather than from scheduled research assessments, so eligibility and follow-up in an emulation partly reflect care-seeking and access rather than the biology the protocol intends. Whether a given database can support a given target trial is a question about the data source and the protocol jointly, and quantitative assessments of that pair now exist. What does not exist is a prespecified stop rule: a threshold agreed before the analysis, on observability, temporal precision, capture continuity, comparator support, outcome validity and linkage completeness, that would halt a study when an essential component fails. Outside tightly gated regulatory and network feasibility processes, most published emulations still assert suitability in a paragraph, and a pipeline with no route to the answer no will always produce an estimate.

Why it is open

Fitness is a property of the pair formed by the data and the protocol, so a fixed score is the wrong object and any threshold has to be estimand- and decision-specific, which makes standardization hard rather than merely unattempted. Validating a threshold against trial concordance is the obvious route and does not work on its own, because the benchmarking corpus is a selected sample of favorable questions. And any threshold that would stop a study costs someone a publication, while no external body currently imposes one.

What has been tried

Work What it contributes
Wang, et al. An operational target trial emulation framework for causal inference using electronic health records. npj Digit Med 2026 Sets out the operational constraints electronic health records place on each protocol component, which is the raw material a fitness score would formalize.
NICE real-world evidence framework States that some target trials cannot be adequately emulated with available data and that bespoke data collection may be preferable, which is the design-failure outcome this problem wants to make operational.

Probable solution or research direction

Build trial-component fitness scores computed against the specific protocol rather than against the database in the abstract, and state the decision they serve, so the threshold is defensible for that decision rather than universal. Predefine stop rules and publish the fitness report whether or not the study proceeds, which is what turns a negative assessment into a citable result. Validate the scores against concordance while stating what that validation cannot establish, since a corpus of emulable questions cannot calibrate a rule meant to identify inemulable ones.

Verification

Verdict. Overstated. 2 independent auditors judged the claim as stated too strong. Many target-trial emulations lack prespecified, protocol-specific quantitative adequacy criteria and no-go rules. Transparent component-level assessments could improve decisions about whether to proceed, but thresholds must be estimand- and decision-specific, and randomized-trial concordance alone cannot validate them.

The source said. “Electronic health records arise from encounters, billing and documentation rather than from scheduled research assessments, so eligibility and follow-up in an emulation partly reflect care-seeking and access rather than the biology the protocol intends. Whether a given database can support a given target trial is therefore a question about the data source and the trial jointly. In practice this is answered narratively, in a paragraph asserting that the data are suitable, with no quantitative thresholds and no procedure that could return the answer no. A pipeline that cannot conclude that the trial is not emulable will always produce an estimate, and the estimate will not carry the fact that the question could not be asked.”

What was wrong with it. Quantitative data-quality assessment for emulation exists and is being formalized, including an assessment of record and linked-claims data built for emulation studies specifically, so the claim that the question is answered only narratively was too strong. What is absent is narrower: prespecified stop rules, and a route by which a negative assessment becomes a published result rather than a private decision.

Dissent. literature dissented (supported-with-caveat): The entry says data-fitness assessment is narrative with no quantitative thresholds. A 2026 JAMIA Open paper assesses the quality of electronic health record data and linked claims data specifically for target trial emulation studies, which is a quantitative fitness assessment for this purpose and not a narrative one. Regulatory syntheses for GLP-1 therapies and oncology reviews also set out data-adequacy considerations. What none supplies is the predefined stop rule the entry asks for, or the publication of a negative fitness assessment as a citable result, so the operational half of the claim stands and the descriptive half is too strong.

GPT-5.6 Sol, technical and source-code lens
open / overstated. The residual problem is real: data fitness is specific to the protocol, estimand, and decision context, and prespecified component-level no-go criteria would improve study governance. The statement nevertheless overstates the absence of quantitative assessment. Availability, temporal resolution, treatment support, follow-up continuity, outcome capture, and validation performance can all be measured, although they do not collapse into a universally valid score. External validation data are not required for every fitness component. A pipeline without explicit stop rules also does not necessarily always return an estimate, while an estimator’s standard error generally will not represent bias from nonidentification or poor measurement. Benchmark concordance cannot by itself validate a fitness score because disagreement can arise from estimand differences, transportability, adherence, measurement, or bias in either study. This is decision-critical when an essential protocol component is unobservable or unsupported; otherwise, standardized scoring and publication of assessments are primarily governance and reproducibility improvements.
Grok 4.5, recency lens
partially-solved / overstated. Inverted-prior search finds substantial prior art that already structures protocol-paired data fitness and can return no, so the claim that fitness is only a narrative paragraph with no thresholds and no procedure that can stop a study is overstated. SPIFD (2021) and SPIFD2 (2023) are step-by-step feasibility frameworks that pair research question, target-trial articulation, and data-source selection, with component-level documentation and heatmaps intended to support go or no-go decisions before estimation. The CARE oncology work (Levy et al., 2026) runs a systematic multi-stage fitness screen over six commercial EHR sources and 23 candidate RCTs: of 54 RCT-RWD combinations, nine reached detailed feasibility and only three were selected for emulation protocol development, with explicit fitness constraints (complex eligibility, biomarkers, performance status, outcome ascertainment). OHDSI DataQualityDashboard implements quantitative pass/fail thresholds on CDM conformance, and DARWIN EU study phases include a feasibility assessment that issues a recommendation on whether to conduct the study. Lee et al. (2026) quantifies TTE-relevant completeness, accuracy, plausibility, and concordance for an EHR cohort against Medicare claims, and Wang et al. (2026) formalizes when EHR realization of trial components can or cannot yield interpretable estimates. What remains open is exactly the hard part of the proposed agenda: field-standard, protocol-component fitness scores with pre-specified stop rules that academic pipelines cannot bypass; routine publication of negative fitness assessments as citable results; and empirical validation that low fitness predicts poor concordance with the corresponding trial. Regulatory and industry pathways already gate more often than the report implies; general-purpose TTE software (TrialEmulation and related packages) still does not enforce a fitness gate that returns no. Cites 10.1002/cpt.70310: Demonstrates a systematic multi-stage, protocol-paired fitness assessment that rates RCT design elements for availability, missingness, and validity and that stops most candidate emulations: 54 RCT-RWD combinations narrowed to nine detailed assessments and three selected for protocol development.. Cites 10.1002/cpt.2883: Provides a structured, documentable process that pairs target-trial articulation with data-source fitness assessment and is intended to identify when design and data are not decision-grade before estimation proceeds.. Cites 10.1093/jamia/ocab132: Implements quantitative data-quality checks with configurable decision thresholds (OHDSI DataQualityDashboard) that can return pass or fail; covers CDM structural and value-level quality rather than full protocol-component TTE fitness, but disproves the claim that no thresholded procedure exists.. Cites 10.64898/2025.12.22.25342844: Quantifies TTE-relevant data quality (completeness, accuracy, plausibility, concordance) for eligibility, exposure, covariates, outcomes, and follow-up in EHR-only versus claims-linked data; shows a quantitative fitness evaluation against a reference source, while still requiring that external validation source..
