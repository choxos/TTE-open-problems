The method landscape

What each approach identifies, where it breaks, and how to choose

The catalog is organized by problem. This page is organized by method: which estimator answers which question, what each one assumes, and where each one fails. It exists so that an entry in the catalog can be read against the method it constrains.

ImportantThe distinction that organizes everything

The decisive division is point treatment versus sustained strategy, not weighting versus regression versus machine learning.

A point treatment is assigned once. One exchangeability condition, one propensity model and a standard estimator suffice, and the classical toolkit transfers with little modification.

A sustained strategy is a rule applied over time, and treatment at each visit is chosen partly on the basis of variables that treatment itself has already affected. That single fact, treatment-confounder feedback, breaks conditional adjustment: a covariate that must be adjusted for as a confounder of later treatment is simultaneously a mediator of earlier treatment, and no regression can condition on it and not condition on it at once. G-methods exist for exactly this and for nothing else.

A more flexible estimator does not move a problem across that line. See LRN-01.

Choosing a method

The branch worth noticing is the one at the bottom. A competing event is not a nuisance to be censored away; censoring at death is itself an identifying assumption, and applying it silently picks an estimand nobody stated. See OUT-01.

Where the bias enters

Read this for what it says about the three inputs no g-method touches.

L1 is the feedback loop the g-methods were built for, and they handle it. U is exchangeability, which nothing in the data can check, and which is why unmeasured confounding carries the largest evidence base in this catalog. V is the visit process: in routine records a covariate is measured when a patient attends care, so L1 is observed selectively, and the standard treatment of that is imputation rather than modeling. See MIS-01. M is that every variable in the diagram is an algorithm over billing codes and clinical text, and enters the analysis as though it were the thing it names. See MER-01.

What each method identifies

Method Setting it is stated for Nominal estimand Main identifying assumptions Principal unresolved weakness
Propensity-score weighting or matching Point treatment, new-user active comparator Marginal contrast in the weighted or matched population Baseline exchangeability; positivity; correct propensity model; consistency The target population is whatever weighting produced, and is rarely stated; no protection against unmeasured confounding
Marginal structural model by IPTW Sustained strategy with treatment-confounder feedback Marginal contrast under each strategy Sequential exchangeability; sequential positivity; correct treatment and censoring models; no interference Weight instability over long follow-up; positivity failures surface as heavy weights rather than as a named unsupported region
Parametric g-formula Sustained or dynamic strategy, discrete visits Counterfactual risk under each strategy Sequential exchangeability and positivity; correct models for every time-varying covariate The null paradox: with a correctly specified but non-saturated model the sharp null can be rejected when it holds; misspecification compounds across time points
Iterated conditional expectation The g-formula setting, long follow-up Same Same, except only the outcome regression at each step must be right Fewer models than the g-formula, and no easier to check any of them
G-estimation of structural nested models Sustained strategy, continuous or dose exposure Effect of one additional unit or period of treatment Sequential exchangeability; a correct model for the effect rather than for the covariates Little software; unfamiliar output; rare in applied emulations
Clone, censor, weight Strategies distinguishable only after time zero, grace periods Per-protocol contrast under each strategy Sequential exchangeability given the censoring covariates; positivity among those still uncensored The grace period is a modeling choice that silently changes the estimand; variance after cloning has no scalable correct default
Sequential nested trials Repeated eligibility, staggered entry Pooled contrast across trials opened at successive times Everything above, plus that pooling across calendar time is meaningful One person appears in many trials; naive standard errors are wrong, and calendar-time confounding is usually unaddressed
Longitudinal targeted learning Sustained strategy with machine-learned nuisances The g-formula estimand Sequential exchangeability and positivity; nuisance convergence rates; correct cross-fitting Cross-validation optimizes prediction, not causal error; flexibility is routinely offered as a substitute for identification
Modified treatment policies Continuous, dose or natural-value interventions Contrast under a shift of the observed treatment Sequential exchangeability; the shifted value stays inside the support Support for the shifted value is exactly the positivity condition, and it is checked far less often than the shift is applied
Doubly robust AIPW and one-step estimators Any of the above The estimand of the estimator being augmented Either the treatment model or the outcome model correct, plus the identification conditions Double robustness protects against misspecification, never against unmeasured confounding or positivity failure
Negative controls, proxies, E-values Any of the above, as a companion A bound, a bias parameter, or a falsification test An assumed structure relating the control to the exposure and the outcome Each answers a different threat, none is integrated with the others, and a passing negative control is not evidence of exchangeability

This table is not a ladder. A doubly robust estimator does not cure positivity failure, a targeted estimator does not cure unmeasured confounding, cross-fitting does not cure a misspecified estimand, and a narrow confidence interval does not establish that the protocol described the trial anyone wanted. Each of those is an entry in this catalog rather than a rhetorical flourish; the summary is GMT-04, which records that no estimator is defensible as a default and that no study is required to show its choice mattered.

No package spans protocol specification, eligibility ascertainment, time-zero alignment, weight estimation, positivity accounting, competing events, measurement error and the reporting a regulator asks for. That gap is itself catalogued, under software, computation and reproducibility.

How to evaluate a method

A simulation study reporting bias without naming the estimand the bias is relative to has reported nothing. In an emulation the estimand is a function of the protocol, so the protocol has to be part of the data-generating mechanism rather than of the analysis.

Metric family Minimum metrics
Estimand recovery Bias and relative bias against a stated counterfactual risk at a stated horizon, RMSE, sign error, and the same quantities under the sharp null
Uncertainty calibration 95% coverage, empirical to nominal standard-error ratio, type I error under the null, and coverage after cloning specifically
Positivity and support Fraction of person-time with no support for the assigned strategy, the weight distribution, the maximum stabilized weight, and the estimate with and without truncation
Time-related bias Sensitivity to the grace period, to the time grid, and to the alignment of eligibility, assignment and follow-up start
Structural robustness Behavior under unmeasured confounding, under an informative visit process, and under phenotype misclassification, varied jointly rather than one at a time
Computation and reproducibility Runtime at national-registry scale, failure rate, seed sensitivity, and whether the analysis reruns from a container

The two rows most often omitted are positivity and structural robustness, and they are the two that decide whether the estimate means anything. Varying one violated assumption at a time is its own catalogued problem (UCF-02): biases can cancel, and a one-at-a-time analysis makes that accident look like robustness.

The last box is the one nobody does. See SFW-03.
