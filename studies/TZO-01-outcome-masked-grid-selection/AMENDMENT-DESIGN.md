# TZO-01 proposed registered amendment

Proposed for registration before any replicate of the amended run is drawn, with a new master seed. It amends `documentation/studies/designs/TZO-01-no-outcome-blind-procedure-selects-the-time-revised.json` and the scaling recorded in `R/00-config.R`. Everything not listed under "What changes" stays as registered, including every decision threshold.

## 1. Why the registered design cannot answer

As registered and implemented, all 48 cells are uninformative, for four separate reasons.

**Zero-initiation weeks make the one-week comparators fail in the higher-pressure scenarios.** In process scenarios 3 and 4, 97 to 100% of 4000-person cohorts contain a weekly-grid week with no initiation (weeks 62 to 104, with 18 to 190 people at risk), and 40% and 7% at the two-week grid. The initiation model carries one intercept per interval over the whole of follow-up, so such an intercept diverges, the Hessian condition exceeds the registered 1e12 and the fit fails. `results/smoke.rds` (one replicate per process scenario, not five) shows every one-week and two-week fit failing in scenarios 3 and 4, which exceeds the registered 5% failure limit for a required comparator in all 24 of their cells. The intercepts that fail are for boundaries after G, and no weight ever uses a fitted probability after G: the delay arm uses boundaries before G and the early arm the boundary at G.

**The early arm lacks support on the one-week grid in the lower-pressure scenarios.** In scenarios 1 and 2 the smoke replicate gives a one-week early-arm effective sample size of 62 to 84 against the registered cell requirement of a median of at least 100. The early arm is supported only by people who happen to start in the interval ending at G, weighted by the inverse of that interval's start probability, so on the one-week grid the effective sample size is set by one week's starters. The `selected`, `one-week-4000` and `full-history` rows all read the one-week fit, so the other 24 cells fail too.

**The selector cannot select anything but one week.** The one-week model also fails in the 1000-person design sample in all four scenarios (smoke: missing cross-validated log loss for the one-week grid in every row), because a 1000-person sample has zero-start weeks late in follow-up even at lower pressure. The selector compares each grid's log loss with the one-week log loss, so with the reference missing no coarser grid can pass and the selector returns one week in every row of the smoke. Its registered success criterion requires at least a 25% reduction in worker time, so selector failure is then an artifact. Separately, the selector's effective-sample-size gate reads the design-sample value (1000 people) against 200, while the registered text calls the quantity "predicted" effective sample size; design-sample early-arm values are 6 to 68 on the one-week and two-week grids (scratch), so even a working selector could not pass the gate at those grids in scenarios 1 and 2.

**The committed truth fails its precision gate in every cell, and `precision_met` says otherwise.** `truth_for_process` stops adding batches when the columns matching `'^(rd-full|approx-panel|approx-oracle|delta-g)'` reach Monte Carlo SE 0.0005, but the approximation columns are named `approx|panel|...` and `approx|oracle|...`, so the pattern never matches them and truth stopped at two batches (500,000 people) with `precision_met` TRUE. In `results/truth.csv` the approximation-error SE exceeds 0.0005 for at least one grid in 48 of 48 cells (one-week grid: median 0.0014, maximum 0.0058). `05-analyze.R` checks that column separately and would mark every cell uninformative. The gate also cannot be met by more of the same: one-week per-batch SDs near 0.004 need about 60 to 130 batches of 250,000, far beyond the registered cap of 16. The extreme early-arm weights, not outcome noise, drive this, since truth already uses expected event probabilities.

**Also, by design:** 7 cells (7, 8, 11, 15, 16, 47, 48) have a full-history truth within 0.002 of the utility boundary and are uninformative under the registered rule whatever is done. And the `full-history` rows are copies of the `one-week-4000` rows; this amendment does not change that, but the owner may wish to confirm that the weekly panel estimator is the registered exact-order benchmark.

## 2. What changes

1. **Initiation-model window.** Every initiation model (analysis panels, selector fits and their cross-validation folds, the resource-equivalent comparator, and the population models used for truth) is fitted only on boundaries at or before G, with one intercept per such boundary. Covariates that are aliased with those intercepts inside the window are removed, as `glm` does; this occurs for `elapsed_intervals` and `prior_visit` when G is one or two grid steps (G = 4 on the two-, four- and eight-week grids), and does not change the fitted probabilities. The rank check is applied within each cross-validation fold. Reason: the failing intercepts belong to boundaries whose probabilities no weight uses. On the one-week grid the model inside the window is still correctly specified (the generating initiation hazard is complementary log-log in exactly these terms), so the one-week functional is unchanged; coarse-grid functionals change because their shared coefficients are now estimated on the window, which is one reason truth is recomputed.

2. **Analysis sample: 4000 to 10,000 people.** The design sample stays at 1000, so the resource-equivalent comparator has 11,000. Reason: early-arm support on the one-week grid is proportional to sample size (section 3).

3. **Selector effective-sample-size gate.** The "predicted arm-specific effective sample size" is computed as the design-sample value multiplied by 10,000/1000, the ratio of analysis to design sample sizes; the threshold of 200 is unchanged. This is the owner's decision: it follows the registered word "predicted", but it changes what the committed code computes.

4. **Truth.** Fix the decisive-column pattern so that the approximation columns are checked. For the early arm, compute each resolution-specific functional by conditional expectation over the natural start week given the process path instead of one drawn start: for every simulated person, sum the weighted contribution over each possible start week before G (weight 1) and within the interval ending at G (weight the inverse fitted probability), each multiplied by its exact probability, plus the forced start at G. Because the outcome hazard depends on time since initiation in every profile except the exact null, each start week needs its own hazard recursion, up to 13 per person per profile and G. The delay arm, whose weights stay between about 1 and 3, keeps plain Monte Carlo. This is valid because in this mechanism initiation affects neither the latent state, the marker nor visits, so the whole process path except the start week is treatment-independent, and the start week's law given the path is `p_start[k]` times the product of `1-p_start[j]` over earlier weeks. It removes the start-week component of the weight variance that dominates the Monte Carlo error. Before registration is final, check it against plain Monte Carlo at 16 batches on cell 1 (agreement within two Monte Carlo SEs of the plain estimate) and time it. Full-history truths and `DeltaG` are unaffected and are reused.

5. **Replicates: 2000 registered (500 implemented) to 500 per process scenario,** with the 5% failure limit and all Monte Carlo interval rules unchanged.

6. **Truncation sensitivities** (cap at 20; 1st and 99th percentiles) are computed in the first 100 replicates of each process scenario only. Reason: they are labeled sensitivity analyses, carry no decision, and take 31% of replicate time (scratch).

## 3. The gates can pass

Measured in scratch with changes 1 and 3 applied in memory to the committed code (two cohorts per process scenario at 8000 analysis people; nothing written under `results/`):

- **Initiation fits.** 64 of 64 analysis-sample fits valid across the four scenarios, both G and all four grids, condition numbers 83 to 14,836 against the limit of 1e12, including the one-week grid in scenarios 3 and 4 where every registered fit failed. All design-sample one-week fits valid (cross-validated log loss 0.10 to 0.26). One full replicate of scenario 3 at 8000 returned 240 of 240 rows without failure.
- **Early-arm effective sample size, one-week grid.** At 8000: 106 to 171 in scenarios 1 and 2 (median of the eight values about 130), 335 to 745 in scenarios 3 and 4. Scaling linearly (the smoke gave 62 to 84 at 4000, the same ratio), 10,000 gives a median near 160 in the worst scenario, a margin of 1.6 over the registered 100. Delay-arm values are above 135 everywhere.
- **Selector branches.** With the predicted effective sample size scaled by 10, the two-week grid passes the gate in the sparse-visit scenarios (design values 24 to 51 in scenario 2 and 74 to 151 in scenario 4, so 240 or more after scaling) and also passes the aliasing bounds there (0.048 and 0.041 against 0.10), while in the frequent-visit scenarios every grid coarser than one week fails aliasing (0.14 and above). So the selector can return a coarser grid, and can fail to, which makes both the "successful partial solution" and the failure branch reachable.
- **Truth precision.** Under change 4 the start-week component of the early arm, which dominates the one-week column, is integrated out. The coarse-grid columns of the committed truth, whose weights are much less extreme, already have per-batch SDs near 0.0004 in most cells; the registered cap of 16 batches reaches an SE of 0.0005 for any per-batch SD up to 0.002. How many batches the conditional calculation needs is not known until measured, so this is the part of the amendment that must be verified before registration is final, by the plain-versus-conditional check in change 4.
- **Coverage band (0.925 to 0.975) for the selector.** At 500 replicates the 95% Monte Carlo half-width at true coverage 0.95 is 0.019, so the band is reachable.
- **Grid consequence branches.** For a paired expected-estimate difference with replicate-level SD near 0.015 (roughly 0.6 times the one-week estimator SE of about 0.025 expected at 10,000), a six-comparison max-t multiplier near 2.6 gives a half-width of about 0.0017 at 500 replicates. The material branch (interval wholly beyond plus or minus 0.002) is reachable for expected differences of about 0.004 or more, and the committed coarse-grid truths already differ from each other by 0.002 to 0.004 in several cells. The equivalence branch is reachable only where the expected differences are within about 0.0003 of zero; see section 5.

## 4. Cost

The scratch replicate of scenario 3 at 8000 took 164 CPU-seconds with the restricted window, against about 305 as registered at 4000, because the one-week model's parameter count falls from 112 to at most 21 and every influence and gradient matrix shrinks with it. Scaling to 10,000 gives about 205 CPU-seconds with the truncation sensitivities and 141 without. Main run: 4 scenarios by 500 replicates by 141 s is 282,000 s; sensitivities in 100 replicates per scenario add 4 by 100 by 64 s, 25,600 s; together 86 CPU-hours. Truth: population models over at most 13 boundaries instead of 104, plus the conditional-expectation batches, each carrying up to 13 hazard recursions per person for the early arm. This has not been estimated; it is timed by the validation check in change 4, and if main run plus truth exceed 100 CPU-hours the replicate count is reduced before registration, never after results are seen. Main run alone: about 86 CPU-hours. Timings were taken at load averages above 300.

## 5. What the amended study no longer answers

- Grid equivalence in cells whose expected grid differences are small but nonzero: at 500 rather than 2000 replicates the equivalence interval reaches plus or minus 0.002 only when differences are within about 0.0003 of zero, against about 0.0011 at 2000. Those cells will be indeterminate rather than equivalent.
- Truncation sensitivities beyond the first 100 replicates per scenario.
- Coarse-grid functionals defined with post-G data in the nuisance model; the amended functionals use the pre-G window only, which is the only part of any fitted model a weight can use.
- Results describe 10,000-person analysis samples, not 4000.
