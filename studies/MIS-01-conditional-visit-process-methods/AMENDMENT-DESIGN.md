# MIS-01 proposed registered amendment

Proposed for registration before any replicate of the amended run is drawn, with a new master seed. It amends `documentation/studies/designs/MIS-01-the-visit-process-is-treated-as-a-nuisance-t-revised.json` and the scaling in `R/00-config.R`. Everything not listed under "What changes" stays as registered, including every threshold.

## 1. Why the registered design cannot answer

**Support is not the blocker; cost is.** The coordinator's oracle checks (20 datasets each at 1000 people) found the oracle g-formula close to valid in four confirmatory environments: sid 1, 43, 86 and 128, with rd24 bias -0.0007 to +0.0067 and coverage 0.95 to 1.00. Two gate risks remain and are treated in section 3: the ratio of mean model SE to empirical SD was 1.20, 0.93, 1.20 and 1.02, and sid 128 (all-weak, sparse) had rd24 bias +0.0067 with Monte Carlo SE 0.0052.

**Cost, measured.** One dataset of sid 1 at the registered 4000 people, CPU-seconds on this machine (load average 300 to 1000): oracle 13.7, complete record 12.6, LOCF 13.6, observation model 2.7, inverse-intensity raw 38.9, capped 62.1, calibrated 1.4, visit process 52.9, joint latent 33.8, MAR likelihood 16.4; about 250 per dataset without multiple imputation. Imputation adds a completed-data g-formula (about 13 s) per imputation, so about 650 s at the minimum m = 50. The decision rule needs at least 56 of 64 oracle-valid pairs, that is at least 112 environments with 900 valid datasets each, 100,800 datasets: about 7000 CPU-hours without imputation and 25,000 with it, before the 80 descriptive environments, the challenge panel's second analysis variant, and the imputation pilot (100 datasets, two runs each, from m = 50 upward). Even oracle, complete record and LOCF alone, 40 s per dataset, need about 1100 CPU-hours. The implemented scaling (1000 people, 20 replicates) is uninformative by the registered rule, which requires 900 valid datasets per confirmatory environment. Two direct measurements agree with this: the implemented imputation-count pilot (10 datasets at 1000 people, two fits each at m = 50) had not finished after 148 CPU-minutes, and one full replicate of two confirmatory environments at m = 50 had not finished after 45 CPU-minutes; both were stopped.

**Where the time goes.** A scratch split of one g-formula fit: the two regressions take 0.24 s at 1000 people and 0.79 s at 4000; one evaluation of `fitted_intervention_risks` takes 0.22 s at either size, and the registered numerical gradient needs 55 evaluations (27 parameters, central differences), 12 s. So 94% of each g-formula fit at 4000 people is the recursion, evaluated through `stats::aggregate` on a data frame every month, and it does not depend on sample size. The same dataset profile at 1000 people confirms it: oracle 12.5, complete record 11.9 and LOCF 12.5 CPU-seconds, against 13.7, 12.6 and 13.6 at 4000. The same recursion serves the weighted and imputation analyses.

**Cohort size.** The registered design specifies 4000 people; `R/00-config.R` uses 1000 as part of its scaling, and the coordinator's oracle checks were at 1000. Since the dominant cost does not depend on sample size, the scaling bought little.

**One-dataset failures that bear on later gates.** In the sid 1 dataset at 4000, `joint_latent` and `mar_mi` both failed at `likelihood-optimization`, and `iiw_calibrated` at `normalization-or-calibration`. Sid 1 is the all-null profile with gamma_L = 0, where the latent likelihood is flat in gamma_L. At 1000 people the same profile ran the latent likelihoods to their end before failing on the score criterion: 1206 CPU-seconds for the joint model and 1303 for the MAR likelihood, 1077 for imputation at m = 2, while the raw and capped inverse-intensity analyses failed on an unsupported cell. One dataset is not a rate, but the visit-aware adequacy branch requires a convergence lower bound of 0.95, and a latent likelihood that costs 20 minutes and then fails would alone put the registered design out of reach.

## 2. What changes

1. **Implementation, not protocol: an array recursion.** Replace the data-frame recursion in `fitted_intervention_risks` (and its analogues used by the weighted and visit-process estimators) by a fixed-state array recursion over baseline cell, current and lagged severity. It is algebraically the same function, so the registered numerical gradient of the complete recursion is unchanged. It must reproduce the current function to 1e-10 on the 128 confirmatory truth mechanisms and on fitted parameters from 10 datasets before use. Expected cost per g-formula fit at 4000 people: about 1 CPU-second instead of 13 (regressions 0.79 s plus 55 array recursions); Stage 0 measures it.

2. **Cohort size: 4000 people**, as registered. Every oracle number from the 1000-person checks is re-measured at 4000 in Stage 0.

3. **Stage 0, a registered stop rule before any evaluation replicate.** 200 datasets at 4000 people in each of four retained informative environments (gamma_L = 1.386294, gamma_A = 0, observation 0.08) with the four profiles the coordinator checked: all-null, AL0-YLw-YAL0-LLw, ALw-YL0-YALw-LL0 and all-weak. Fit oracle, complete record and LOCF. Stop, and investigate the oracle procedure instead of running, if in any environment (a) the 95% interval for the oracle SE-to-SD ratio for rd24 lies wholly outside 0.96 to 1.07, (b) the 95% interval for oracle rd24 bias lies wholly outside plus or minus 0.0043, or (c) more than 1 of 200 oracle fits fail. These are the ranges inside which the registered oracle-validity gate is passable at 1000 replicates (section 3); they are not new thresholds. Stage 0 datasets are pilot data and are excluded from evaluation, as the registered pilot rule requires.

4. **Grid: 128 confirmatory environments to 64; descriptive panels removed.** Fix gamma_A = 0 and keep the 16 latent-role profiles, both gamma_L levels and both observation frequencies. The strong-coupling, fixed-intercept and challenge panels (80 environments) are removed. Reason: cost; gamma_A = 0 isolates observation that depends on current latent severity, which is the question's object, and the owner may prefer gamma_A = 0.693147.

5. **Methods in the evaluation run: oracle, complete record, LOCF.** Inverse-intensity, calibrated-weight, visit-process, joint-latent and MAR multiple-imputation analyses are removed from the decision. The imputation-count tolerances are not changed; the imputation method is simply not run.

6. **Counts carried as fractions of the halved grid.** With 32 pairs and 32 informative members: at least 28 oracle-valid pairs (56 of 64); LOCF materially inadequate in at least 12 informative members (24 of 64) spanning at least 8 profiles, with at least 4 at beta_AL = 0 and 4 at beta_YAL = 0 (8 of the 32 such members each); paired LOCF deterioration of at least 0.01 in at least 8 pairs (16 of 64) spanning at least 6 profiles. Negative branch: at least 28 oracle-valid pairs; LOCF and complete record adequate in at least 28 informative members spanning all 16 profiles; paired increase below 0.005 in at least 28 pairs; material inadequacy in at most 2 members (4 of 64). Profile-spanning requirements are unchanged, and each profile still has two informative members, so every rule stays satisfiable. Replicates stay at 1000 with the registered minimum of 900 valid datasets.

7. **Fallback if change 1 is not adopted or Stage 0 measures more than 5.5 CPU-seconds per dataset.** Run 8 environments instead: the four Stage 0 profiles, each as a gamma_L pair at observation 0.08 and gamma_A = 0, with the same methods and 1000 replicates. The count rules cannot be carried as fractions when fewer than 8 profiles exist ("spanning at least 8 profiles", "all 16 profiles"); what replaces them is the owner's decision, and without one the fallback is descriptive.

## 3. The gates can pass

**Oracle overcoverage (coordinator's first question).** An SE-to-SD ratio of 1.20 gives coverage 2 Phi(1.96 times 1.20) minus 1 = 0.981; no replicate count puts an interval around 0.981 inside 0.925 to 0.975, so such an environment is excluded from every pair it belongs to. At 1000 replicates the coverage interval (half-width about 0.0135) fits inside the band only for true coverage between about 0.940 and 0.963, that is for an SE-to-SD ratio between about 0.96 and 1.07; at 500 replicates the admissible ratio narrows to about 0.98 to 1.03, which is why replicates are not cut. The empirical SD from k datasets has relative error 1/sqrt(2(k-1)): 0.16 at 20 and 0.05 at 200, so Stage 0 separates 1.20 from 1.00 by four standard errors. Sandwich conservatism of this kind shrinks with sample size, so a ratio of 1.20 at 1000 people does not predict one at 4000; Stage 0 measures it where the run will happen.

**Oracle bias (coordinator's second question).** The gate needs an upper one-sided 97.5% Monte Carlo bound for absolute rd24 bias of at most 0.005. At 4000 people the rd24 SD is about 0.0117 (half the 0.0233 seen at 1000), so at 1000 replicates the Monte Carlo SE is 0.00037 and the gate passes for true absolute bias up to about 0.0043. Plug-in g-formula bias from finite-sample regression bias falls roughly as 1/n, so the +0.0067 seen at 1000 people, if real, should be near 0.002 at 4000. At 200 Stage 0 datasets the Monte Carlo SE is about 0.0008, which decides whether the bias is below about 0.003 or above about 0.006 before any evaluation replicate is drawn.

**Oracle convergence.** The gate needs a lower one-sided 97.5% binomial bound of at least 0.99; at 1000 replicates that allows at most 4 failures. Stage 0 stops at more than 1 in 200.

**LOCF materiality.** At 4000 people the rd24 SE is about 0.011, so an LOCF bias of 0.015 already drives coverage to about 0.72; both the bias bound (lower bound at least 0.02) and the coverage bound (upper at most 0.90) are reachable for moderate bias. The all-null profile, in which severity affects neither treatment nor outcome, cannot make LOCF biased and gives the reachable negative control; in the sid 1 dataset LOCF gave -0.0623 against the oracle's -0.0630. Stage 0 measures LOCF bias in the three nonnull profiles before registration, which is the first measurement of it in any informative environment.

**Negative branch.** Adequacy needs an upper absolute-bias bound of at most 0.01, a coverage interval inside 0.925 to 0.975 and convergence of at least 0.95; with the Monte Carlo SEs above, all three are reachable when LOCF and complete record are nearly unbiased and their sandwich SEs are calibrated to within about 5%.

## 4. Cost

With change 1: about 1.3 CPU-seconds per g-formula fit (0.79 measured plus array recursions, to be confirmed), 4 per dataset including generation. Stage 0: 4 by 200 by 4 s, 0.9 CPU-hours (8.9 with the current code). Evaluation run: 64 environments by 1000 replicates by 4 s, 256,000 s, 71 CPU-hours. Recursion validation: under 1 CPU-hour. Total about 73 CPU-hours; the margin to 100 allows the per-dataset cost to reach 5.5 s. Without change 1 the same run costs about 710 CPU-hours, and the fallback (8 environments by 1000 by 40 s) costs 89 plus 8.9 for Stage 0, about 98 CPU-hours.

## 5. What the amended study no longer answers

- Whether any visit-aware method is conditionally adequate, and how inverse-intensity weighting, calibrated weights, the visit-process g-formula, the joint latent likelihood and MAR imputation compare. The joint and MAR likelihoods also need their optimization failures investigated first.
- Whether observable diagnostics identify replicate-level estimation error: the registered classifier needs at least 30 mixed-class held-out environments and models for the removed methods.
- Monitoring that depends on prior treatment (gamma_A), the original strong-coupling benchmark, fixed-intercept comparisons, and the three misspecification axes.
- Under the fallback: 12 of the 16 profiles and the dense observation frequency.
