# STR-01: a consensus procedure for treatment-version classes, piloted on crystalloid fluids

**Catalog problem.** No procedure defines clinically defensible equivalence classes of treatment versions. Verdict `partially-addressed`; triage: consensus, answerable in part, feasibility 1, scope applies.

**Residual claim this design tests.** The audit located the gap. Estimation under multiple versions of treatment is formalized (VanderWeele and Hernán 2013; Hernán and VanderWeele 2011; Wanis 2019), cloning handles individuals compatible with more than one prespecified strategy (Gaber 2024), and multivariate modified treatment policies estimate an analyst-specified joint policy; codex added that none of these chooses the strategies, that per-protocol estimation does not require artificial censoring and weighting, and that observed data can expose version heterogeneity but cannot establish that versions are equivalent. Grok found no constructive procedure in guidance, software or the 2024 to 2026 literature. GLM noted that a general criterion is the wrong shape because clinical sameness depends on drug, indication and era. What remains open, and what a study can test, is whether a defined procedure (an outcome-blind inventory of the versions actually given, a structured clinical consensus on their classes, and a support check before any outcome is seen) produces classes that are reproducible across panels and whose unresolved choices can be bounded in their effect on the answer. The triage asks for one clinically bounded pilot; this is it.

**Why crystalloids.** A 2026 emulation of balanced crystalloids versus 0.9% saline in MIMIC-IV (n = 42,883), with external validation in eICU-CRD, found that two prespecified exposure definitions, initial assignment and the dominant strategy over 48 hours, gave directionally opposite estimates for major adverse kidney events within 30 days (odds ratios 0.49 and 2.51), with treatment switching in 89.4% of MIMIC-IV patients against 1.2% recorded switching in eICU-CRD (Dai 2026, abstract). The versions differ by fluid type, volume, sequence, bolus versus maintenance use, drug-diluent volume and mixtures of both fluids, which covers the entry's dose, formulation, combination and adherence dimensions. A randomized comparison of the same strategies exists (Semler 2018) for context, not as a test of the classes. Both databases are obtainable without fee after PhysioNet credentialing. The owner may substitute another indication; every stage below transfers unchanged.

## Questions

**Q1.** Can a clinical panel reach consensus on which crystalloid versions belong to a balanced strategy, a saline strategy, neither, or a separate strategy, and which dimensions stay unresolved?

**Q2.** Does an independent second panel, given the same inventory and items, arrive at the same classes?

**Q3.** How do treatment-history support (strategy compatibility, dual compatibility requiring cloning, effective sample size) and the per-protocol risk difference change across the consensus classification, its alternatives from unresolved items, and the two definitions used in the 2026 emulation?

## What this leaves unanswered

Whether the consensus classes are clinically correct: agreement and stability are not validity, and the triage is explicit that estimate stability is a sensitivity result. A general criterion for other drugs or indications. The bias of a wrong class relative to the positivity cost of the right one, which the entry proposes to settle by simulation and which is a separate study.

## Design

### Stage 0: outcome-blind version inventory

A data analyst with no access to outcome tables extracts from MIMIC-IV (Johnson 2023; release current at extraction, recorded by version number) and eICU-CRD (Pollard 2018) every crystalloid administration in the first 72 hours of eligible ICU stays. Eligibility is fixed by the steering group before extraction, following the SMART population of critically ill adults as closely as the databases allow. For each stay the inventory records fluid product (0.9% saline, lactated Ringer's, multiple-electrolyte solution, dextrose-containing and hypotonic solutions, other), volume, bolus or continuous use, whether the volume is a drug diluent or carrier, timing relative to ICU admission, and pre-ICU fluid where recorded. It then summarizes versions as the patterns that occur: balanced share of crystalloid volume over 24, 48 and 72 hours; first fluid type; sequences (saline then balanced, balanced then saline, alternating); diluent share. Each pattern carries its frequency in each database. The outcome variables for major adverse kidney events (death, new renal replacement therapy, persistent renal dysfunction) are withheld from the analyst by extract design until stage 3.

### Stage 1: main panel, modified Delphi

Panel: intensivists (10), critical care pharmacists (4), nephrologists (3) and emergency physicians (3), from at least four countries and six institutions, recruited by invitation and nomination; target 20, floor 12 completing round 3; two methodologists attend as nonvoting members. With 15 raters one panelist moves an agreement percentage by about 7 points.

Items, drafted by a steering group of three from the stage 0 inventory, one per membership-deciding rule: for each version pattern, the class it belongs to for the question "does a balanced-crystalloid strategy reduce major adverse kidney events at 30 days relative to saline" (balanced, saline, neither and censored, separate strategy); the balanced-volume share that constitutes adherence; whether diluent and pre-ICU volumes count; the grace period after ICU admission within which the first fluid does not define assignment; which deviation ends adherence. Each rule is rated on a 1 to 9 scale for each candidate class or threshold, with a free-text rationale. Rounds 2 and 3 show the group distribution and anonymized rationales, with each rule's frequency in the inventory so panelists see how many stays a rule moves. Ten rules are repeated verbatim in round 3 to measure test-retest agreement. Agreement thresholds, fixed before round 1: a class or threshold is adopted when at least 70% rate it 7 to 9 and at most 15% rate it 1 to 3. A rule without an adopted option after round 3 is unresolved, and each of its two most-supported options generates an alternative classification. Reported under ACCORD (Gattrell 2024), with the consensus definition criteria of Diamond (2014).

The output is a versioned classification: a table from every inventory pattern to a class, each entry labeled adopted or unresolved with its agreement, frozen and time-stamped before stage 3.

### Stage 2: replication panel

A second panel of 8 to 10 clinicians with no member in common with the first, drawn from different institutions, rates the same items with the same materials, blind to the first panel's results. Between-panel agreement is Cohen's kappa over the class assigned to each version pattern, weighted by the pattern's frequency in MIMIC-IV, and the share of eligible stays whose strategy membership differs between the two panels' classifications. The inventory is expected to yield on the order of 40 patterns; at that size a kappa near 0.8 carries a standard error of roughly 0.07, enough to separate the 0.80 and 0.60 thresholds below.

### Stage 3: support and stability, after freezing

Classifications compared: the main panel's; one alternative per unresolved rule; the replication panel's; and the two definitions of the 2026 emulation as closely as its published text allows. For each, in both databases: the share of eligible stays compatible with each strategy, the share compatible with both for part of the grace period (the cloning burden), and the effective sample size of each arm under the fixed censoring-weight model. Then one fixed per-protocol analysis: clone, censor at deviation, inverse-probability-of-censoring weights from a pooled logistic model in 6-hour intervals over the first 72 hours with a prespecified covariate set, risk difference for the 30-day outcome with a nonparametric bootstrap interval (500 resamples of stays). The estimator is held fixed because the question is the classification, not the estimator. Stability is the range of risk differences across classifications relative to the width of the main classification's interval.

## What would show the problem real or not real

- **Real, for this indication**: the replication panel's classification differs from the main panel's for at least 10% of eligible stays, or between-panel weighted kappa is below 0.60; and the risk differences across the panel-derived classifications (main, unresolved alternatives, replication) span more than twice the width of the main classification's 95% interval. A defined procedure then does not yield a class that another panel would reproduce, and the choice it leaves open changes the answer.
- **Not real, for this indication**: consensus is reached on at least 90% of membership-deciding rules, weighted by the stays they move; between-panel kappa is at least 0.80 with at most 5% of stays reclassified; and every panel-derived classification's estimate lies within the main classification's interval. The procedure is then sufficient here, and the catalog's claim reduces to the absence of the procedure in general, which this design supplies for one case.
- **Premise narrowed**: consensus and replication succeed but the estimate still spans more than twice the interval width across the 2026 emulation's definitions and the consensus classes. The disagreement in the published emulation then came from definitions a clinical panel would not adopt, which is itself the practical content of the entry.
- **Uninformative**: fewer than 12 main panelists or 6 replication panelists complete round 3, or test-retest agreement on the repeated rules is below 0.60, in which case ratings are too unstable to define a class.

## Cost

Person-hours: credentialing and data access 10; stage 0 extraction and inventory 80; steering group, item drafting and round materials 60; recruitment and administration 40; Delphi analysis 30; stage 3 analysis in two databases 80; write-up 40. About 340 staff hours, plus panelist time of about 4 hours each for 30 panelists (120 hours, typically honoraria). Compute: censoring weights are modeled in 6-hour intervals over the 72-hour window in which adherence is defined, about a million cloned person-interval rows per database; a logistic fit of that size takes a few CPU-seconds, so 500 resamples for each of at most seven classifications in two databases (7,000 fits, plus the outcome models) need about 10 to 25 CPU-hours; the budget is 40. Data access: PhysioNet credentialed access to MIMIC-IV and eICU-CRD (training certificate and data use agreement, no fee). Ethics: expert surveys usually need exemption or minimal-risk approval; the database analysis is covered by the data use agreements.

## Threats to validity

Panel composition shapes the classes; the replication panel measures, rather than removes, that dependence. The inventory frames the items, so a version the extraction misses cannot be classified; the diluent and pre-ICU fields are the likeliest omissions and their completeness is reported by database. Panelists see how many stays each rule moves, which is intended, but a panelist aware of the published directional conflict may favor classes that resolve it; outcome data are withheld and the published estimates are not shown. One indication in one era of two databases: the procedure transfers, the classes do not. The fixed estimator cannot remove confounding by institutional fluid practice, which the 2026 emulation identified as a threat of its own.
