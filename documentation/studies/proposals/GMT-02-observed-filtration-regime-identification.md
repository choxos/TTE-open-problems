# GMT-02: which continuous-time dynamic regimes survive treatment-dependent visiting

**Catalog problem.** Identification of dynamic regimes in continuous time under treatment-dependent observation is unresolved. Verdict `partially-addressed`; triage: analytic, answerable in part, feasibility 1.

**Residual claim this design tests.** The audit narrowed the entry. One auditor (codex) held that treatment-dependent visiting does not by itself destroy identification, and that a regime effect is generally not identified when clinically relevant covariate history between visits is unobserved and no observation model is imposed; it rejected the entry's claim that there are no natural sample-size asymptotics, and this design does not revisit that claim. The literature auditor found estimation apparatus that already handles irregular, informative visit times (continuous-time marginal structural model weights from a marked point process, Kalia 2025; continuous-time g-computation compared with inverse weighting, Chatton 2021) but no identification theorem stated in filtration terms for dynamic regimes. Neither opinion says which regimes are identified when the visit intensity depends on unrecorded health and on treatment. That is the gap this design closes for one explicit class of processes: it separates regimes whose decisions use only recorded information, which it claims are identified however informative the visiting is, from regimes that prescribe assessment times or act on the unrecorded state, which it claims are not identified without an assumption about the visit process that the data cannot check.

## Question

For a continuous-time process in which a latent clinical state is recorded only at visits, treatment can change only at visits, and the visit intensity may depend on the latent state and on treatment history:

1. Is the outcome risk under a dynamic regime that sets treatment at each visit from recorded information, with visits left to occur as they would, identified from the observed-data law? Under which conditions?
2. Is the risk identified under a regime that prescribes assessment times, or that responds to the latent state as it changes? If not, which added condition restores identification?
3. What does the discrete-grid g-formula with last-observation-carried-forward covariates estimate, and at what rate does it approach the target of question 1 as the grid refines?

## What this leaves unanswered

Estimation theory (efficiency, rates for flexible intensity estimators, finite-sample positivity) is not studied; one continuous-time targeted estimator for monitored processes already exists (Rytgaard 2022). Treatment that changes between visits, as pharmacy dispensing records it, is excluded by assumption A0 below. Measurement error at visits, censoring other than the outcome, competing events and interference are out of scope. Whether electronic health records satisfy the conditions is an empirical question the theorems cannot answer: the condition that separates the identified from the nonidentified regimes (visits independent of the unrecorded state given the recorded past) is untestable from the observed law, and the study says so rather than proposing a test. Grid-refinement stability is treated as a sensitivity analysis, not an identification check, following the audit.

## Setting and assumptions

Horizon τ. Baseline covariates W, recorded. A latent state X(t) with values in a finite set, càdlàg. A visit counting process N^V with intensity ℓ_V(t, X(t−), H(t−)), where H(t−) is the recorded history; the dependence on X(t−) and on the treatment history in H is unrestricted. At a visit at time v the value L(v) = X(v) is recorded without error. Treatment A(t) ∈ {0, 1} is piecewise constant. An outcome counting process N^Y has intensity ℓ_Y(t, X(t−), A(t−), W). The recorded filtration F_t is generated by W, N^V, the marks L(v) at visits, A and N^Y. No two processes jump at the same time.

- **A0.** Treatment changes only at visit times.
- **E (consistency).** Under a regime, the observed processes equal their counterfactual versions up to the first departure from the regime.
- **SE (sequential exchangeability with respect to F).** At a visit v, the treatment decision kernel π(a | full history) depends on the full history only through (H(v−), L(v)). No decision uses unrecorded information.
- **P (positivity).** For every visit reachable under the regime, π(d_v(H(v−), L(v)) | H(v−), L(v)) > 0.
- **V-MAR (monitoring at random).** ℓ_V(t, X(t−), H(t−)) does not depend on X(t−).
- **V-POS.** ℓ_V is bounded below by a positive constant on [0, τ].

Three regime classes, each fixing treatment by rules d_v:

- **G_rec, recorded-information regimes.** At each visit, A(v) = d_v(H(v−), L(v)); the visit process is not intervened on. Example: start treatment at the first visit with L(v) = 1 and continue.
- **G_mon, monitoring regimes.** Assessments at prescribed times s_1 < s_2 < ... and A(s_k) = d_k(recorded history, X(s_k)); natural visits continue alongside the prescribed assessments, and treatment changes only at the prescribed assessments. Example: assess every 3 months and start if the state is 1.
- **G_lat, latent-state regimes.** Treatment responds to X(t) as it changes. Example: start within δ of the first entry of X into state 1.

## Claims to be proved or refuted

**Claim 1 (identification of G_rec).** Under A0, E, SE and P, for every regime in G_rec the law of the recorded process under the regime is absolutely continuous with respect to the observational law, with density process

  Z(t) = ∏_{visits v ≤ t} 1{A(v) = d_v(H(v−), L(v))} / π(A(v) | H(v−), L(v)),

which is F-measurable. The regime risk is therefore E[Z(τ) 1{T ≤ τ}], and equals the product-integral g-formula built from the observed-data intensities of visits, marks and outcome with the treatment kernel replaced by d. The claim holds with no restriction on how the visit intensity depends on X(t−) or on treatment history. Proof route: the full-data likelihood factorizes into latent-state, visit, mark, treatment and outcome kernels; the regime replaces only the treatment kernel; under SE its likelihood ratio is F-measurable; a change of measure by an F-measurable density leaves the conditional law of the latent path given the recorded path unchanged, so every observed-data intensity is the same functional of the recorded history under both measures. This extends the change-of-measure argument for continuous-time marginal structural models (Røysland 2011) to marked visit processes that depend on a latent state.

**Claim 2 (nonidentification of G_mon and G_lat).** Without V-MAR, the risk under a nontrivial regime in G_mon or G_lat is not identified. Construction to be written out and verified: the latent state is the pair (K, X(t)), with a type K ~ Bernoulli(π). Under law P, X(t) = K for all t. Under law P′, X(t) = K + (1 − K) 1{t ≥ τ/2}. Under both, visits occur at rate λ when K = 1 and never when K = 0, treatment starts at the first visit, and the outcome intensity is h_0 exp(β A(t−)) with β ≠ 0. The recorded laws coincide, yet the regime "start treatment when X(t) first equals 1" gives risks that differ by (1 − π)[F_never(τ) − F_{τ/2}(τ)], where F_never is the risk without treatment and F_u the risk when treatment starts at u: under P the K = 0 people are never treated, under P′ they start at τ/2. The same construction with a single prescribed assessment at 3τ/4 gives the G_mon case. The construction violates V-POS as well as V-MAR, which is why Claim 2b is needed. Claim 2b, which the study must settle rather than assume: nonidentification persists when every individual can be seen (ℓ_V = λ_0 + λ_1 X(t−), λ_0 > 0) in the nonparametric model for the latent process; a proof or a counterexample in which the intensities are identified from the recorded law is required.

**Claim 3 (restoration).** Under V-MAR and V-POS, regimes in G_mon are identified: the conditional law of X(s) given H(s−) equals the mark law at a visit at s given H(s−), so the g-formula with the mark kernel evaluated at the prescribed times identifies the risk.

**Claim 4 (discretization).** The discrete-grid g-formula with width δ, covariates carried forward from the last visit and treatment decisions at grid points, targets a grid regime whose risk differs from the G_rec risk of Claim 1 by O(δ) when all intensities are bounded; the leading constant is a product of the treatment effect on the outcome intensity, the visit rate, and the decision-to-effect delay the grid imposes. It does not converge to a G_mon or G_lat risk unless V-MAR holds.

## How the result is checked

A two-state model (X ∈ {0, 1}) over 24 months with every quantity computable exactly:

- Latent transitions: rate q_01 = 0.05 exp(−0.7 A) per month into state 1 and q_10 = 0.02 back.
- Visits: ℓ_V = 0.25 exp(ν_X X + ν_A A) per month, with ν_X ∈ {0, log 3} (latent-dependent visiting off or on) and ν_A ∈ {0, log 2} (treatment-dependent visiting off or on): four settings.
- Treatment at visits: start with probability 0.6 if L(v) = 1 and 0.05 if L(v) = 0; once started, continue.
- Outcome: h = 0.01 exp(1.1 X + log(0.6) A) per month.

For the G_rec regime "start at the first visit with L(v) = 1", the triple (X, A, alive) is a finite-state continuous-time Markov chain under the regime, so the exact risk is a matrix exponential. For the G_mon regime "assess at months 3, 6, ..., 21 and start if X = 1", the exact risk is a product of matrix exponentials with deterministic jumps. The checks, each written as a separate program from the exact calculation:

1. **Claim 1.** Simulate the observational process for 10^6 people by event-driven competing exponentials and compute the Hajek estimate with density Z(τ) using the known treatment kernel. In all four settings, |estimate − exact| must be below 3 Monte Carlo standard errors (the standard error is of order 0.001 at this size and is reported with each comparison). A discrepancy in the setting ν_X = log 3 with the code verified in the setting ν_X = 0 is a counterexample to Claim 1.
2. **Claims 2b and 3.** Apply the observed-data g-formula that assumes V-MAR to the G_mon regime. It must match the exact risk within 3 Monte Carlo standard errors when ν_X = 0 and differ from it when ν_X = log 3; report the difference.
3. **Claim 4.** Fit the grid pooled-logistic g-formula at δ ∈ {3, 1, 1/2, 1/4, 1/8} months on 10^6 simulated people, so that estimation error is small next to discretization error, and regress log |bias| on log δ; the slope must be near 1 against the G_rec target.

## What would show the problem real, not real, or leave it open

- **Real in the entry's strong form.** A counterexample to Claim 1: two laws satisfying A0, E, SE and P with identical recorded laws and different G_rec risks, or a check 1 discrepancy in a setting satisfying the assumptions that survives independent re-implementation. Treatment-dependent observation would then block identification even for regimes that use only recorded information.
- **Not real for recorded-information regimes.** Claim 1 proved and check 1 passing in all four settings. Treatment-dependent and even latent-dependent visiting would then not block identification of G_rec; the open part of GMT-02 narrows to regimes that prescribe assessment or act on unrecorded state, which Claims 2 and 3 tie to the untestable condition V-MAR. This is the expected outcome, and it is a substantive narrowing, not a null result: every applied analysis that states its regime in G_mon or G_lat terms (for example "treat when the marker exceeds a threshold") while its data follow G_rec timing is estimating a different regime.
- **Uninformative.** Neither a proof nor a counterexample for Claim 1 or Claim 2b is completed, or the exact and simulated calculations disagree in a setting where no claim is at stake (a code error that is not resolved).

Both substantive branches are reachable: the proof route for Claim 1 rests on the invariance of the latent posterior under an F-measurable change of measure, which fails if any treatment kernel in the construction leaks latent information, and the counterexample search targets exactly that.

## Cost

Person-hours: step 0, reading Lok 2008, Zhang 2011, Røysland 2011, Rytgaard 2022 and Røysland 2025 to record which of Claims 1 to 4 each already covers (the Zhang title names the setting of this proposal; its coverage must be established before any claim is presented as new), about 16 hours; proofs of Claims 1, 3 and 4 and the construction for Claim 2, 60 to 100 hours for someone who works with counting-process causal inference; exact calculations and simulations, 20 hours; write-up, 10 hours. Compute: the matrix exponentials take seconds; the event-driven simulations are 4 settings by 2 regimes by 10^6 people plus five grid fits, under 5 CPU-hours on the shared machine, estimated from about 20 events per person and vectorized draws. No data access is needed.

## Threats to validity

- **Prior coverage.** Claim 1 may already be a special case of Røysland 2025 or of the identification route in Rytgaard 2022. Step 0 decides this; if so, the contribution is Claims 2 to 4 and the explicit statement that latent-dependent visiting is permitted under SE.
- **A0 is strong.** Many records capture dispensing between visits. Relaxing A0 adds a treatment counting process with its own intensity; Claim 1 should extend when that intensity depends only on recorded history, but the proof is not attempted here.
- **Finite latent state.** The proofs must not use it; only the checks do.
- **The regime classes may not match applied protocols.** Each regime in the checks is written out as a protocol sentence, and the write-up states for each which class it belongs to.
- **Positivity with rare visits.** Under P, a regime whose decisions fall at rarely visited histories is identified but not estimable with useful precision; the checks report the effective sample size of Z(τ) alongside every comparison.
