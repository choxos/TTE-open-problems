# Themes the gap labellers wanted and could not find

Twelve labellers attached 798 future-research gaps to a fixed set of 40 themes, working on
disjoint gaps and unable to see one another. 167 gaps landed on no theme. Each labeller was
asked at the end what it had wanted and not found.

Six absences were named by more than one labeller. That is independent agreement about a hole
rather than one model's opinion, which is the only reason these are worth working up at all.
Absences named once are recorded below the table and are not carried forward.

These are candidates and nothing more. Unlike the proposals that became entries, they have had
no reading and no second review: nobody checked the papers behind them, and nobody asked whether
each is a gap for the field or a to-do for the authors who wrote it. One of them, the software
absence, was already proposed during the reading and rejected by the second reviewer on the
ground that software availability is a research-practice constraint rather than an
identification problem. Three labellers reaching it again from different gaps is new evidence
about how often the field says it, not a reversal of that judgment.
